# Louisiana Commercial Lease Law: What the Operator Tracks, and What Counsel Decides

> Context, not counsel. This article is not legal advice, not a statute summary you may rely on, and not a procedure for evicting anyone or seizing anyone's property. Louisiana commercial lease enforcement has to be read by a lawyer licensed in Louisiana, against the current Code and the signed lease, before you send a notice, change a lock, hold property, or file anything. Day counts, article numbers, and remedies below are the topics the source draft raised. They are held here so a reviewer can check them. They are not instructions.

Owners who learned leasing in a common-law state bring habits that Louisiana does not share. The Civil Code, not a memory of "what leases usually do," is the backdrop for a Louisiana commercial lease. Contracts still matter. Statutory rules on notice, possession, and the landlord's rights in property on the premises can sit on top of the form you signed, and sometimes they limit what the form can waive. The operator's work is to know which clauses to put in front of counsel, and to keep the file that counsel will need. The operator's work stops before self-help.

This article sits in The Owner's Contracts Series beside the obligation worksheet and the notice discussion in "The Clauses That Create Recurring Work." It is the jurisdictional reading list for a Louisiana file. "Tenant Default and the Louisiana File: Notice, Possession, and the Empty Bay" is the operating sequence that follows a missed payment. Read them together, and send both to counsel before you use either one.

## Start with the jurisdiction, not with a national form

A lease written on another state's form can look complete and still miss the Louisiana questions: which notices the Code requires even when the lease is silent, which waivers the Code will not honor, how a guaranty behaves when you later amend the lease, and what you may not do to the space while a default is unresolved. Those are drafting and enforcement questions. They are cheaper to ask while the lease is still a draft than after a tenant has stopped paying.

The operating habit is simple. For every Louisiana lease, the file contains the executed document, every amendment, the notice addresses as they stand today, and a short list of questions for Louisiana counsel. The list is not a memorandum of law written by the office. It is the sections you could not read with confidence, each with its number.

Operator habit, stated as a file rule: when the lease is in Louisiana, local counsel reads it before you send a default notice, and again before you draft a form you plan to reuse. A review at signing costs less than a procedural restart.

## The lessor's privilege is a file problem and a counsel problem

The source draft describes a lessor's privilege under Louisiana Civil Code article 2707 and following: a right, arising by operation of law, in movables on the leased premises, securing rent and other lease obligations, and described there as broader than a lien that exists only if the lease creates it. The same draft says the privilege can be lost if those movables leave the premises without the landlord's consent, and that a court process — a writ of sequestration — is how a landlord asks the sheriff to hold property, usually after posting a bond.

Treat that paragraph as a topic list, not as a statement of your rights.

What the operator does while counsel is engaged:

- Note, in writing, what is on the premises and whether anyone is preparing to remove it. Photographs and a dated log are the record. They are not a seizure.
- Do not detain property, block a vehicle, or confront a tenant to "preserve the lien." If removal is underway, call counsel and follow counsel's instruction.
- Ask counsel, with the lease attached, what article 2707 and the following articles require today, whose property they reach, and what you must not do before a court order.

A privilege you enforce yourself is how a rent claim becomes a damages claim against the landlord.

## Monetary default: the notice file, then counsel on acceleration

When rent is late, the lease's notice clause and the Code both have to be satisfied. The source draft's operating picture is a written notice that states the amount and the cure period, sent even when the lease says notice is waived, because a paper trail matters later. Many commercial leases also contain an acceleration clause that purports to make the remaining rent due on default. The same draft says acceleration depends on invoking it the way the lease describes, including saying so in the notice if that is what the clause requires.

The operator's piece is the file:

| Item | What to capture | Who decides what it means |
|---|---|---|
| Amount | Rent, recoveries, and other sums, each tied to the ledger and the clause | Counsel, if the tenant disputes the number |
| Address and method | The notice clause as written, and the address you will actually use | Counsel, if the method you use is not the method named |
| Cure window | The days the lease states, and the day you treat as day one | Counsel, before you treat the window as expired |
| Acceleration | Whether the lease has the clause, and whether this notice mentions it | Counsel drafts or approves the sentence that invokes it |
| Proof of sending | Certified mail, receipt, courier proof, or hand delivery, as the clause allows | The file keeps it; counsel uses it |

A demand that misstates the amount or uses the wrong method can force the sequence to start over. Have counsel read the letter before it goes out. The series article on dates and notice windows is the calendar habit. It does not supply Louisiana notice law.

## Possession and money can be different cases

The source draft distinguishes two judicial paths, and the distinction is the one to put in front of counsel rather than to choose alone.

It describes a rule to vacate under Code of Civil Procedure article 4701 and following as a summary proceeding aimed at possession: a notice to vacate (the draft says five days, or a shorter lease period that the draft says may not go below twenty-four hours), then a rule asking the court to order the tenant out. The draft says this path is often faster and often does not carry the full money claim with it.

It describes an ordinary proceeding as the slower path that can combine possession with past-due rent, a claim for accelerated rent, and damages.

The business question you can answer is which outcome you need first: the space back, or one case that also seeks the money. The legal question counsel answers is which proceeding is available on these facts, what the notice must say, and what the current articles require. Do not file either one from this article.

## Sequestration is a court process

The source draft describes a writ of sequestration under Code of Civil Procedure article 3571 and following as a pre-judgment remedy: the landlord petitions, posts a bond set by the court, and the sheriff seizes and holds movables on the premises so they remain available for the rent claim. It is aimed at the moment when property is about to leave.

That is counsel's petition, counsel's bond discussion, and the sheriff's act. It is not a landlord inventory you conduct by changing the locks. Ask counsel whether the writ is appropriate, what the bond will require, and what you must preserve in the file. Then wait.

## Force majeure is the clause you signed

Fortuitous event, in the Civil Code's language, is the doctrine the source draft points to when performance becomes impossible because of an unforeseen and irresistible event. The draft's practical warning is about the clause, not the headline: many force majeure provisions exclude the duty to pay rent. A tenant's argument and a landlord's response then turn on the words in that lease, including leases rewritten after the pandemic years.

Read your clause into the worksheet. Quote what counts as the event, what performance is excused, and whether rent is inside or outside the excuse. Ask counsel before you grant or refuse an abatement. A verbal "we'll work it out" is how the clause gets replaced by a habit.

## A guaranty is a second document

The source draft treats personal guaranties under Louisiana suretyship rules as strictly construed, with ambiguity read in the guarantor's favor, and with a risk that a lease amendment made without the guarantor's consent can affect the guaranty. Enforcement, in that draft, follows the path of the underlying obligation, and the guaranty's value depends on execution, a clear description of what is guaranteed, and any waivers the guarantor actually signed.

The operator's rule is mechanical. Every amendment goes to counsel with the guaranty attached, and the question is written down: does this change require the guarantor's signature or a reaffirmation before you rely on the guaranty? Do not assume the guaranty "still covers it" because the tenant signed the amendment.

## What to hand counsel before a form is reused

Boilerplate imported from another state is a known failure mode in the source draft. The drafting list to send with the form:

- **Default.** What counts as monetary default, what counts as non-monetary default, the notice, the cure, and whether acceleration is stated.
- **Waiver of notice.** Which notices the lease attempts to waive, and which ones counsel says cannot be waived.
- **Fees and costs.** Whether the lease provides for attorney's fees and court costs, in the form counsel will stand behind.
- **Indemnity.** What the tenant indemnifies, and what it does not.
- **Maintenance.** Who repairs what, in words a vendor and a tenant can both be pointed to.
- **Notices.** Address, method, and copies, matching the way your office actually sends mail.

A Louisiana-specific form, reviewed by Louisiana counsel, is part of opening the center for leasing. It is not a template to download and edit in the office.

## Questions operators ask, and the answer that stays inside the file

**May I change the locks?** No. Do not change locks, remove property, or shut off utilities to force a commercial tenant out. The source draft cites a statutory prohibition and warns of damages, including for the tenant's business. Counsel confirms the citation and the remedy. You do not test the prohibition while that confirmation is pending.

**Does a privilege reach all property on the premises?** The source draft says the privilege is broad and can reach movables of the tenant, a sublessee, and in some cases a third person. That is a legal conclusion about someone else's property. Ask counsel before you treat any item as covered, and do not hold the item yourself.

**How fast is a possession case?** The source draft says a rule to vacate can finish in weeks if nothing goes wrong, and that an ordinary case takes longer. Your timeline is whatever the court and the facts produce. Plan the vacancy as a range, and let counsel tell you what is realistic this month.

**Is a verbal commercial lease enough?** The source draft says verbal leases can be enforceable and that a commercial term longer than a year generally needs a writing for the terms you will want later. Use a written lease. Ask counsel what your unsigned deal actually is before you act as if it were the form you meant to sign.

**How long do I have to sue on a written lease?** The source draft points to a ten-year prescription period and compares it to a statute of limitations. Confirm the accrual date and the period for the claim you have. A citation in a chart is not a calendar entry.

## The practical next step

1. For each Louisiana lease, confirm the file holds the executed lease, every amendment, the guaranty, and the notice address you would use today.
2. Write a one-page question list for Louisiana counsel: privilege, default notice, acceleration, possession versus a money claim, sequestration, force majeure, and the guaranty. Attach the sections. Do not attach your conclusions.
3. Adopt the self-help rule in writing for the team: no lock changes, no property removal, no utility shutoffs, unless counsel has a court order in hand and has told you the next step.
4. Send the national form you have been reusing, if you have one, to Louisiana counsel before the next deal is negotiated on it.
5. Keep this article at review until that counsel pass is done. Then correct the citation table to what counsel confirms, or delete the rows counsel will not stand behind.
