# Pull the variance and the easement before you count stalls

Floor area that cannot be parked cannot be leased, and a curb cut you do not control is not a front door. Both facts rest on the public record: a variance, a plat, a parking or access servitude, sometimes a restriction that survived the contract that created it. This note is the checklist an operator runs against the recorder and the planning file. It does not restate what a stall count means for a lease, and it does not inventory throat widths. It says which instruments to pull and which numbers to copy.

Copy what the page says. Do not translate a variance condition or a survival clause into a legal conclusion. If a sentence will change what you lease, the copy goes to counsel with the instrument attached. The folder's job is to hold the instrument and the dates.

## The public-record checklist

Work in this order. Each item ends as a PDF in one property folder, named by instrument number, or as an open item with a person and a date. "We think we have it" is not a status.

1. **Plat.** The recorded plat the surveyor filed, including the latest revision noted on its face. Copy the stall count the plat stripes, the "not a part" parcels, and the easements drawn on the face. A site plan traced from an aerial is not this document.
2. **Parking variance.** The recorded variance or ordinance the planning file points to. Copy, verbatim, the required count, the provided count, and any condition that ties floor area, seats, or use to parking. Copy the instrument number and the date. If a later amendment exists, it is a separate PDF, not a rumor.
3. **Parking and access servitudes.** Every reciprocal easement, shared-drive agreement, and parking agreement the plat's exceptions list names. For each: parties, what is shared, who maintains it, any stalls you rely on and whose land they sit on, expiry or renewal mechanics, and any sentence that says a right survives termination. Copy the exhibit that draws the servitude area. If the exhibit is not in the download, the item stays open. Do not draw the shared aisle from memory and call it the exhibit.
4. **Use restrictions that ride along.** Liquor waivers, prohibited uses, and consent rights that live inside an easement rather than a lease. Copy any survival language. Draw the line on the site plan only from the exhibit, and label the drawing as traced from that exhibit on a stated date.
5. **Three counts, three sources.** Stalls the variance says are provided, stalls the plat stripes, and stalls a servitude lets you count that are not on your land. They are allowed to disagree. Mixing them is how a lease assumes parking the record cannot deliver. An end date that a count depends on goes on the critical-dates calendar with a review at least a year ahead.
6. **Open items.** Every instrument on the exceptions list that you have not pulled, and every missing exhibit, gets a row: instrument number, who is pulling it, by when. An easement nobody has read is an easement the leasing plan does not understand.

In Louisiana the conveyance index at the parish clerk and the planning department's variance file are the two public places this list starts. Ask for the instrument by number. A handwritten note of what a closing attorney once said is not a pull.

## What the checklist refuses to do

It does not approve a restaurant, and it does not say a shared driveway is yours. Once a year, and again before any lease that changes use or intensity, a person re-opens the three counts and the expiry list and checks them against the PDFs. A fresh look at the exceptions list is how you find a recording you did not have last year. Until that check, the folder is last year's knowledge.

**Illustrative row, not a recorded instrument.** A hypothetical corner servitude, call it Entry EXAMPLE-000, states that a center may count a handful of stalls on a neighbor's parcel and that the right ends on a date certain. The checklist row holds the entry number, the stall sentence copied verbatim, the expiry, and a flag that the exhibit PDF is still outstanding. Nobody leases the end-cap against those stalls while the exhibit flag is open. The number of stalls is whatever the instrument says when it is pulled. It is not a number this note invents.

A model can list dates and instrument numbers from a downloaded PDF and draft the reconciliation table. A person pulls the record, files the exhibit, and decides whether counsel reads a survival clause before the next letter of intent.

## The next step

1. Copy the exceptions list from the recorded plat onto one sheet. One row per instrument.
2. Pull the variance and every parking or access servitude the list names. File them by instrument number.
3. Write the three stall counts with their sources. Leave a blank rather than a guess.
4. Calendar every expiry the instruments state, with a review at least a year ahead.
5. List exhibits not yet in the folder as open items with a name and a date. Pull them this quarter.
