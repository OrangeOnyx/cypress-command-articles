# The food tenant runs on four calendars

A restaurant, a quick-service counter, and a shop that fries anything share a problem the rest of the rent roll does not. Their right to keep cooking depends on paper that expires: a pump manifest, a suppression-system tag, a health permit, a fire inspection. Each one is issued by a different party, on a different cycle. The lease may say the tenant handles "all compliance." The operator still needs to know which of the four is current, because a dark hood or a backed-up interceptor becomes a common-area problem, a neighbor problem, and a leasing problem the week it fails.

This note is not a code treatise. It does not say what a parish requires, what a lease obliges, or whether a given kitchen is in compliance. It says which four documents an operator should be able to put a hand on, and what to do when one is missing: ask, date the ask, and route a real compliance question to the inspector, the service vendor, or counsel rather than guessing from memory.

## Four rows, one stack

Keep one stack per food tenant. Four rows are enough. Each row has the same columns: item, last date, next date, who holds the paper, where the PDF lives, status.

- **Grease.** Interceptor or trap, whichever the suite has. Last pump date, the vendor, the next date the vendor or the lease schedule calls for, and the manifest or invoice in the folder. Who pays is a lease question. Whether the manifest exists is an operator question. A verbal "we pumped it last month" is not a row.
- **Hood and suppression.** The service tag on the equipment and the report in the folder should be the same visit. Write the company, the date on the tag, and the next service date the tag or the contract states. If the tag in the kitchen and the PDF in the folder disagree, the status is question, not current.
- **Health.** The permit or the latest inspection result, the office that issued it, and the expiration or next inspection date if one is printed. File the tenant's paper here. Do not file the owner's certificate of occupancy in its place and call the row done.
- **Fire.** The inspection or acceptance that covers the suppression system, separate from the health row. Where the owner holds common-area life-safety inspections, those stay on the owner's calendar, not on the tenant's stack. Mixing them hides a gap in one or the other.

In Louisiana, parish sanitarians and the fire officials who look at hoods do not share one calendar. A passed health inspection is not a passed suppression inspection. Copy the dates you see. Do not infer one from the other.

Status on each row is current, due soon (inside sixty days), requested, missing, or question. "Missing" means you asked the folder and it was not there. "Requested" means you asked the tenant or the vendor and wrote the date. Leave the legal reading of a missed pump or a lapsed tag to the people who do that reading.

## Cadence, including the dark kitchen

Glance at every food stack once a quarter. Glance monthly at any row inside sixty days. The chase matches the certificate chase: one request, a second request, then a question row rather than a silent lapse. Send the request to the tenant and, when you have it, to the vendor named on the last manifest or tag. One row, two dates. Do not open a new stack because a new manager answered the email.

A kitchen that has gone dark is still a food stack until the use in the file changes. Note a lapsed tag or an unpumped interceptor on the soft-vacant row if you keep one, and keep the four dates alive. When a new food user is proposed, the stack joins the leasing screen: grease path, hood, and which of the four papers would have to be created rather than renewed. The rent is hypothetical until those answers are in the folder.

**Illustrative stack.** A hypothetical end-cap restaurant, Unit D, has a pump manifest from last quarter and a suppression tag that expired in the spring. The health permit in the folder has a date that has passed; nobody has asked for the renewal. The fire row is blank. Monday's work is three requests, three dates on the existing rows, and no speech about whether Unit D is "a good operator." Friday's work is filing whatever came back and marking the rest question. The code book stays on the shelf.

A model can read dates off a manifest or a tag photo and flag a blank row. A person asks for the missing paper and decides who reviews a conflict between the tag and the lease.

## The next step

1. List every tenant whose use cooks, fries, or discharges grease. One stack each, four rows, even where you expect a blank.
2. Put the newest manifest, tag, permit, and fire paper you can find in the folder, or mark the row missing.
3. Send this week for anything missing, expired, or inside sixty days.
4. Put the quarterly glance on the same calendar as certificate review, and name the person who owns both.
