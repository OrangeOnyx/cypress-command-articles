# Redline the option, then put the notice on a calendar

Keeping a good tenant is cheaper than replacing one. The renewals article in the Shopping Center Operator Series builds the calendar: expiration, a conversation a year or more ahead, and every option deadline. This note is the markup that has to happen before those dates mean anything. An option you have not read is a date you have guessed.

The work is a pen on the executed lease and three rows on the calendar. It is not a second essay on retention. The lease's sentences decide method, timing, and rent. Counsel reads them when the consequence is in dispute. The operator copies them in time to calendar the result.

## What gets the pen

Open the option section of the executed lease in the folder, not the draft in the drop slot. Copy the words. A paraphrase such as "six months' notice, market rent" is how a formula becomes a round number nobody signed.

- **Window.** The first day a notice may be given and the last day it must be sent or received. "Received by" and "mailed by" are different rows. Copy the verb the section uses.
- **Method.** Certified mail, overnight, personal delivery, email, a portal — whichever the section names. A text message is not a method you invent.
- **Address.** The notice address, and any amendment that changed it. The address on the tenant's website is not the notice address unless the lease says it is.
- **Who may exercise.** Copy the party as named. A storefront trade name is not the party.
- **Rent rule.** Fixed steps, a percentage, an index, fair market, the greater of two tests. Copy the formula and the exhibit it points to. Do not convert it into a dollar figure in the margin. A missing exhibit means the redline is unfinished.
- **Late or early notice.** Copy the sentence that says what happens outside the window. Do not translate it into "void" or "still good." If the section is silent, write "not stated here" and leave the reading to counsel.
- **Conditions.** Continuous operation, no outstanding default, a guaranty still in place. Mark each as stated or not stated in the option section. The markup does not decide whether a late payer has lost the right.
- **Proof.** Whatever the section treats as evidence of sending or receipt. The proof goes in the lease folder with the notice, or the notice is not in the file.

Do this when the lease is filed, and again when an amendment touches term, notice, or rent. The folder's index line should say whether the old option section survived.

## Three dates, one status

For each option, the calendar gets three dates and a status the monthly review can say in one breath.

- **Opens.** The first day of the window. Before it, do not file an early letter as if it were the exercise.
- **Chase.** A date you pick inside the window, not a date the lease creates. For a tenant you hope will stay, it is the conversation the renewals article already calls for. For a tenant you expect to use a formula, it is a reminder to watch the notice address. The chase date does not create a right.
- **Deadline.** The last day the section states, alarmed far enough ahead that a missed week is not a missed window.
- **Status.** Not yet open. Open, no notice. Notice in file. Late or early notice, question. Exercised, document in file. "Notice in file" means the notice, the proof the method requires, and the date, in the lease folder. An email that says they "plan to renew" is a conversation note unless the section you copied says it is an exercise.

When the deadline passes with no notice in the file, the status becomes question — not "option dead," and not "still open." The copied sentence, and counsel if the consequence is disputed, do that work. Negotiating as if an unexercised option still caps the rent is how the calendar fails in public.

**Illustrative markup.** A hypothetical inline lease, Unit E, says the tenant may renew by notice given inside a stated window before expiration, by certified mail to the notice address, at a rent "as set out on Exhibit R." The redline copies the window, the words "certified mail," the address, and a pointer to Exhibit R. It does not compute a rent. The calendar gets the open date, a chase date inside the window, and the deadline. Status today: not yet open. The shape is the lesson. The figures are not a real lease.

A model can propose the three dates from sentences you paste in. A person checks them against the executed PDF and is the only one who changes the status when a notice arrives.

## The next step

1. Pull every executed lease with an option. If the PDF is not in the folder, stop and fix the file before you calculate a date.
2. Copy window, method, address, party, and rent rule onto one row per option. Leave blanks blank.
3. Put open, chase, and deadline on the same calendar as expirations. Set status for each row.
4. In the monthly renewal pass, read the status column. Any row still paraphrased from memory gets re-copied from the PDF before the meeting ends.
