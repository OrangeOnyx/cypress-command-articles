# The first five days after an ACH return

ACH does not fail the way a card fails. You submit a debit, the register moves to submitted, and for a day or two the cash may even look settled. Then the tenant's bank sends it back. Ordinary reasons, insufficient funds, a closed account, an account the bank cannot find, often land within a few banking days. That one-to-five-day stretch is this note. The books are cheapest to correct then, and the tenant can still fix the month.

A claim that the debit was not authorized can arrive much later. Do not retry it, and do not treat it as a late fee. Stop, and hand the authorization and the lease to the person who owns disputes. This note ends at that edge. What a formal notice must say is the lease plus counsel. The day the return posts, run one sequence, the same way every time.

## Counted is not cleared

If your register marks a draft "received" on the day you submit it, a return will corrupt the delinquency list in both directions. You will think a late tenant has paid, or you will forget to reopen a row you already closed. "ACH or Card: How Multi-Tenant Rent Actually Clears" makes the receipt provisional until your window passes. This note is what you do when the window produces a return.

On the day the return appears:

1. **Reverse the receipt the same day.** The rent is unpaid again. Do not leave the row received and "remember" the return at month-end. Do not net it against next month's draft in your head. This month's row stays this month's row.
2. **Put the unit on the open list,** even if grace has not ended. The return is a fact. The late fee is a separate decision, made only if that lease's grace has ended and you would have charged any other unpaid suite the same way. The note "Late fees and grace periods that survive a file review" is the fee rule. Do not invent a harsher one because the payment bounced.
3. **Read the reason you were actually given.** Write it on the row in plain words. Insufficient funds is not "default." Account closed is not something you cure by submitting the same debit again this afternoon.

A return fee is an expense, or a charge to the tenant only after someone checks that the lease allows it. Do not fold it into the rent because the processor billed you.

## One path, then stop stacking

Reply to the tenant with the amount, the day, and the reason you were given. Offer one replacement path that you wrote down in advance. A workable house path looks like this. Change it to match your counsel and your leases, then stop improvising.

| Reason you were given | What you do in this window | What you do not do |
|---|---|---|
| Insufficient funds | Ask for a replacement by a date you name: a new debit only after they confirm the account, or a check or wire if that is your stated alternative | Do not fire a second debit the same day to the same account unless your written rule allows exactly one retry |
| Account closed, or no such account | Stop the draft. Ask for new payment details in writing, or take the other method you already allow | Do not retry the old details |
| Tenant says the debit was not authorized | Stop. Do not re-present. Hand the file to the dispute owner | Do not email a legal theory, and do not assess a fee as punishment for the dispute |

One replacement, if your rule allows it, is a process. Three attempts in a week can turn a collections problem into an authorization problem. If the replacement fails, the suite is simply unpaid and joins the ordinary pass the day after grace.

The tenant note is short: amount, date, the reason you were given, the one replacement path, the date you need it. A model can draft it from the row. A person sends it. The model does not decide to retry.

If the original debit already moved into the operating account, the return will pull it back or short the next deposit. That is the payout problem in "When a Single-Asset LLC Should Pay Itself by Hand." By the end of the day the register and the bank should match: the rent is open, and the cash is not spent.

## The practical next step

1. Change the register so a submitted ACH cannot show as received until your return window has passed with no return.
2. Write the three-row path above onto the bottom of the rent register, edited to what your leases and counsel actually allow.
3. The next return: reverse it the day it posts, tell the tenant the reason you were given, and take only the one replacement path.
4. Anything that says "not authorized" goes to the dispute owner the same day, with no retry.
