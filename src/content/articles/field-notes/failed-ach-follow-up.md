# The first five days after an ACH return

ACH does not fail the way a card fails. You submit a debit, the register moves to submitted, and for a day or two the cash may even look settled. Then the tenant's bank sends it back. Ordinary reasons, insufficient funds, a closed account, an account the bank cannot find, often land within a few banking days. That one-to-five-day stretch is this note. The books are cheapest to correct then, and the tenant can still fix the month.

A claim that the debit was not authorized can arrive much later than five days. Do not retry it, and do not treat it as a late fee. Stop, and hand the authorization and the lease to the person who owns disputes. This note ends at that edge. What a formal notice must say, and by when, is the lease plus counsel. The day the return posts, run one sequence, the same way every time.

"ACH or Card: How Multi-Tenant Rent Actually Clears" is the decision to use the rail and the moment a draft counts as collected. "One Payment Link per Unit, Without a Full Property System" is the register the return has to hit. This note is the week the register is wrong unless you touch it.

## The window, day by day

The days below are an illustration, not a network timetable and not a promise that a return will arrive on day three. Processors differ. Banks differ. What does not differ is the order of the work: the receipt stays provisional, the return reopens the rent, and you take one replacement path.

Imagine a placeholder tenant, Harbor Goods LLC, on unit label C-2. Total rent this month is $2,400. None of this is a live account.

| Day | What you see | Status on the register | What you do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0, Monday | You submit the $2,400 debit from the row | Submitted. Not received. | Nothing else. Do not call it paid. Do not assess a fee. |
| 1–2 | The processor may show the debit as settled | Still submitted, until your window says otherwise | Leave it. Settlement on the processor's screen is not the same as kept. |
| 3 | A return posts: insufficient funds. The $2,400 is coming back. | Returned, then open. Rent unpaid. | Reverse the receipt the same day. Tell the tenant the reason you were given. |
| 4 | The tenant confirms the account, or sends another method | Open, with one replacement in flight if your rule allows it | One path only. A new debit after they confirm, or a check or wire you already allow. |
| 5 | The replacement has or has not arrived | Received, only if the replacement has cleared your rule. Otherwise still open. | If it failed or never came, stop. The suite is unpaid. It joins the ordinary list. |

Day 0 is where the row gets closed too early. If you mark C-2 received on Monday, Wednesday's return has to unwind a closed row, a sweep you may already have made, and a delinquency list that no longer includes them. Days 1 and 2 are the quiet part. The balance looks fine. The cushion in "When a Single-Asset LLC Should Pay Itself by Hand" exists for that quiet. If the $2,400 already moved to the operating account, the return pulls it back or shorts the next deposit. Day 3 is the day you fix the books, not day 30. A late fee, if one is coming, waits for the grace clause in "Late fees and grace periods that survive a file review." Days 4 and 5 are one attempt. If the replacement clears, the row is received for the amount that cleared. If it does not, C-2 is unpaid and the weekly pass picks it up the day after grace.

## Counted is not cleared

If the register marks a draft received on the day you submit it, a return corrupts the delinquency list in both directions. You will think a late tenant has paid, or you will forget to reopen a row you already closed.

On the day the return appears, three entries happen before anyone debates tone.

1. **Reverse the receipt the same day.** The rent is unpaid again. Do not leave the row received and "remember" the return at month-end. Do not net it against next month's draft in your head. This month's row stays this month's row. In the illustration, rental receipts for the month go down by $2,400. The open balance goes up by $2,400. There is no new income and no new fee in this entry.
2. **Put the unit on the open list,** even if grace has not ended. The return is a fact. The late fee is a separate decision, made only if that lease's grace has ended and you would have charged any other unpaid suite the same way. Do not invent a harsher fee because the payment bounced.
3. **Read the reason you were actually given.** Write it on the row in plain words. Insufficient funds is not "default." Account closed is not something you cure by submitting the same debit again this afternoon.

A return fee from the processor is an expense, or a charge to the tenant only after someone checks that the lease allows it. In the illustration, a $5 return fee stays a $5 expense until that check is done. Do not fold it into the $2,400 because the processor billed you. A tenant who asks what they owe should be able to see rent, and then a fee if the document allows a fee, as separate lines.

The books, the same afternoon, should read like this. Rent billed for C-2: $2,400. Cash collected for C-2: $0. Processor return fee: $5, on the expense line or in a holding note until the lease is checked. Status: open. That is a clean reversal. A note that says "they paid, but there was a problem" is not.

## One path, then stop stacking

Reply to the tenant with the amount, the day, and the reason you were given. Offer one replacement path that you wrote down in advance. A workable house path looks like this. Change it to match your counsel and your leases, then stop improvising.

| Reason you were given | What you do in this window | What you do not do |
|---|---|---|
| Insufficient funds | Ask for a replacement by a date you name: a new debit only after they confirm the account, or a check or wire if that is your stated alternative | Do not fire a second debit the same day to the same account unless your written rule allows exactly one retry |
| Account closed, or no such account | Stop the draft. Ask for new payment details in writing, or take the other method you already allow | Do not retry the old details |
| Tenant says the debit was not authorized | Stop. Do not re-present. Hand the file to the dispute owner | Do not email a legal theory, and do not assess a fee as punishment for the dispute |

One replacement, if your rule allows it, is a process. Three attempts in a week can turn a collections problem into an authorization problem. If the second debit also returns, you do not send a third. The suite is unpaid. It joins the ordinary delinquency pass.

The tenant note is short enough to send the same day. For the illustration: we submitted $2,400 on Monday; the bank returned it for insufficient funds; this month's rent is unpaid; please replace it by Friday by the one method already agreed, a new debit after you confirm the account in writing, or a check. It does not say they are in default, quote a statute, or offer a payment plan. A model can draft that note from the row. A person sends it. The model does not decide to retry.

If the reason is already "not authorized," you do not collect a replacement story. You stop, and the authorization in the lease file goes with the handoff. A partial replacement is labeled as partial. If $1,000 arrives on day 4, the row is $1,000 in and $1,400 open. It is not a new payment method for the lease, and next month's draft is a separate decision.

## What you leave for the ordinary week

By the end of day 5 the special window is over. Either the rent is replaced and the row is received under your clearing rule, or it is open and the weekly pass owns it. Do not keep a side list of "ACH problems" that never joins the delinquency list. Side lists are how a returned draft misses the call on the day after grace.

The log line is one sentence. Unit C-2, $2,400 returned on day 3 for insufficient funds, one replacement requested, outcome on day 5. That sentence is what the Friday review reads, and what explains the receipt two months later. The processor's report is the evidence. The sentence is the decision. A model can flag a return that has not been reversed by day's end. It cannot choose the replacement path, and it cannot tell a tenant they are late.

## The practical next step

1. Change the register so a submitted ACH cannot show as received until your return window has passed with no return.
2. Write the three-row path onto the bottom of the rent register, edited to what your leases and counsel actually allow, including whether one retry is permitted.
3. The next return: reverse it the day it posts, tell the tenant the reason you were given, and take only the one replacement path.
4. Book any processor return fee on its own line until someone confirms the lease allows you to bill it.
5. Anything that says "not authorized" goes to the dispute owner the same day, with the authorization and the lease, and with no retry.
