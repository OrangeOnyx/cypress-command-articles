# Soft vacant is occupied on paper and dark on the walk

An empty storefront is not one fact. A dark bay can be truly vacant and available, owner-used and not for lease, or under a letter of intent and off the market. That classification belongs on the rent roll before anyone prints a flyer. Soft vacancy is a different problem, and it is easy to miss, because the roll is not wrong in the way a mislabeled vacancy is wrong.

The lease is in force. Rent may still be arriving. The suite is dark: hours posted and unkept, inventory gone, mail stacking inside the glass. The occupancy line still says occupied. If you shop the space, you may be offering a suite the file does not yet allow you to offer. If you leave the line alone, leasing waits on a tenancy that is already gone in every way a customer can see. The operator's job is a status and a chase, not a vibe.

## A status the roll can say out loud

Add one status beside occupied, vacant, owner, and LOI: **soft vacant**. It means four things at once, and the file should show each of them.

- **Lease in force.** There is an executed lease on file, or the log honestly says executed, not in file. You do not invent an end date.
- **Operations dark.** Someone walked it. The note says what they saw and the date. A tenant's promise to "reopen next month" is a sentence in the note, not a reason to skip the status.
- **Money state.** Current, late, or on a payment plan. Write the fact. Do not fold it into a story about whether the tenant is "fine."
- **Shop / do not shop.** Do not shop, until the file contains whatever your lease and your counsel say you need before the space is offered. The status can still drive prep: measurements, photos of the shell, a list of what a replacement would need. Prep is not a listing.

Soft vacant is not a fourth kind of empty box you add to a brochure. It is an occupied line with an operations flag. Brokers should not receive it as availability. The leasing owner should not be allowed to forget it. Both mistakes come from using one word, vacant or occupied, for two facts.

## The chase is the hygiene

Soft vacancy rots when it lives in a text message. Give it a row: unit label, date the dark condition was first noted, last walk, last conversation, rent state, and the next action with a date and a name. The next action is usually one of three: call again, send a written question to the notice address in the lease, or take the file to counsel because the lease's default or surrender language is now the question. This note does not decide which of those the lease requires. It decides that the question has an owner and is not still "someone should look into Unit C."

Walk the center on the usual route and treat a dark occupied bay as a finding, the way a burned-out light bank is a finding. Photograph the glass. File the photo with the row. A photo from six weeks ago and a photo from this week are how you tell a slow week from a closed business.

Keep the rent roll honest in the other direction too. If the tenant reopens, the status comes off the same day, with a note. If the tenancy actually ends — keys, a signed surrender, a judgment, whatever your file and your counsel treat as the end — the status becomes vacant, and only then does the suite join the available list. Skipping that step is how a center advertises a space it still has a tenant in.

**Illustrative row.** A hypothetical inline service tenant, call it Unit C, paid last month and has not opened in three weeks. The executed lease is in the folder. No one has sent a notice. The roll stays occupied. A new row says soft vacant, names the person who walked it on Tuesday, and sets Friday as the day that person either reaches the tenant or escalates the question. The flyer does not change. The chase does.

A model can list bays whose sales-tax permit, posted hours, or last work order suggest nobody is operating, if you have those records. A person walks the glass and is the only one who sets the status.

## The next step

1. On the next walk, mark every occupied bay that is dark. One row each. No availability change yet.
2. Confirm the executed lease is in the folder before you write anything about term or default.
3. Assign the next action and a date. Conversation, written question, or counsel — pick one, and write it.
4. Review the soft-vacant rows in the same monthly pass as expirations. A row with no date is the row that will still be there next quarter.
