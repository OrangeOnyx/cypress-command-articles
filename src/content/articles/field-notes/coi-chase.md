# Chase the certificate before the policy lapses

A certificate of insurance is a snapshot. It describes the policies in force on the day an agent issued it. Tenants and vendors renew, change carriers, and change agents on their own calendars. None of that reaches the owner unless someone asks. A property system will nag. A folder and a one-page tracker will too, if one person owns the cadence and the definition of "in file" is strict.

## What "in file" means

A certificate is in the file only when every line below is true.

- The PDF, or a scan of the paper, sits in that party's folder. An email attachment, a portal link, and a photo on a phone are pointers, not the file.
- The named insured matches the tenant or vendor on the lease or contract, character for character. A personal name in place of the company is not a match.
- The certificate holder is the owner entity the contract names.
- Effective date and expiration date are copied onto the tracker, not left for someone to re-read the PDF next month.
- The date you asked and the date the certificate arrived are both written on the same row.

A certificate from last year, still the only PDF in the folder, is in the file and it is stale. A certificate "somewhere in the inbox" is not in the file at all. The tracker should be able to say which problem you have. It should not say that the limits are enough. Limits, additional-insured wording, and whether a lapse is a contract problem are questions for your insurance advisor and, where the document's consequence matters, for counsel. The tracker decides only that the paper in front of you is this party's current paper.

## A cadence that does not need a PMS

One sheet. One row per party. Columns: party, role (tenant or vendor), expiration, request sent, received, status. Status is one word from a short list: current, due soon, requested, lapsed, question.

- Ninety days before expiration, the row becomes due soon. You do not have to send yet unless that party is historically slow.
- Sixty days out, send the request to the producer named on the current certificate and copy the party. Write the date in "request sent." Do not open a second row for the same party.
- Thirty days out with no reply, send again and note the second date on the same row.
- The week of expiration, still nothing: status becomes lapsed. The note changes. You are no longer asking ahead of a date. You are asking because the certificate you hold has ended. Say that plainly. Do not add a sentence about default.
- The day a PDF arrives, check name, holder, and dates before you file it. If they line up, status is current, and the old PDF moves to an archive folder inside the same party folder so nobody grabs last year by accident. If they do not line up, status is question. Coverage questions go to the insurance advisor. Contract-wording questions go to counsel. The owner decides what to do with the answer.

Before lapse, the chase is a calendar. After lapse, the chase is a gap. Mixing those two sentences in one email is how requests get ignored. The row keeps them separate. Vendors and tenants use the same columns. The only difference is who you copy: the producer on the certificate, and the person who signed your contract.

**Illustrative week.** A hypothetical neighborhood center keeps this sheet on a shared drive and nothing else. Unit B is a local service tenant whose certificate expires on the 15th of next month. The landscaper's certificate expired six weeks ago; one request went out, and the row still says requested. On Monday the operator sends Unit B's sixty-day ask and a second ask to the landscaper, and writes both dates. On Friday a certificate arrives for the landscaper. The insured name is an individual's name, not the company on the service contract. The row becomes question, not current. Two sends, two dates, one mismatch a person can see. That is the system.

A model can lift dates and names off a PDF and draft the ask. A person sends the ask, files the reply, and owns the question row. The model does not decide that coverage is sufficient, and it does not send.

## The next step

1. List every tenant and vendor whose contract mentions insurance. One row each, even when you are sure the paper is fine.
2. Put the newest certificate you can find in that party's folder, or write "not in file."
3. Send this week for anything expired or inside sixty days.
4. Name the person who opens the sheet on the first Monday of the month, and put that Monday on the same calendar as the rent.
