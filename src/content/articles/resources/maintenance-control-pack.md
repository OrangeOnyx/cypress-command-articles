# Maintenance Control Pack

Takeover, inspection, preventive calendar, work-order priorities, vendors, capex, and the daily walk. Work the list below. Progress saves in this browser. [Download the printable blank](/resources/maintenance-control-pack.md).


<div class="ix" data-ix="checklist" data-id="res-maint"></div>

A blank operating pack for one shopping center. It ties four things together: what is on the property, what you do before it fails, what you do when it fails, and how you explain the spend. Print it. Keep one copy at the desk and one with whoever walks the lot.

This is operator context. It is not an engineering opinion, a code opinion, or legal advice. The lease decides who repairs what. A statute or a permit decides what must be tested. When those conflict with habit, write down the conflict and ask the person whose job it is to answer it.

Fill the header once, then work the sections in order on a takeover and on the calendar after that.

## 1. When to open this pack

- [ ] Takeover: sections 2 and 3 inside 30 days of appointment
- [ ] Ordinary weeks: section 5 (calendar), section 6 (priorities), section 11 (walk)
- [ ] Money: section 8 and section 9 at the quarterly owner review, and again at the annual plan
- [ ] A loss or an after-hours failure: section 10 first, then the vendor list in section 7

## 2. Takeover protocol

Do these before you promise a tenant a repair you have not seen.

### Thirty days before, or as soon as you have access

- [ ] Prior work-order history exported, including anything still open
- [ ] Vendor list with contract end dates and certificate end dates
- [ ] Roof, HVAC, fire, and lot warranties located, not described from memory
- [ ] As-built drawings compared with one walk. Note every mismatch
- [ ] Keys, codes, and alarm accounts inventoried. Plan the change for day one
- [ ] Open capital jobs named, with who pays and who is on site

### Fourteen days before

- [ ] After-hours roster confirmed: HVAC, electrical, plumbing, roof, board-up, glass
- [ ] Utility account numbers and meter locations written down
- [ ] Known leaks, trip hazards, and dark poles listed with a photo
- [ ] Tenant contacts for facilities, not only for rent

### Seven days before, and day one

- [ ] Alarm and after-hours test placed on the calendar
- [ ] Vendors told, in writing, who may approve work and who may not
- [ ] First walk scheduled with the outgoing manager, every service yard included
- [ ] Photo set of each elevation, the lot, and the roof access stored with the date

Use four words, the same way every week. Times below are a starting point to agree with ownership. Write the times you actually promise, then keep them.

| Priority | Examples | Acknowledge | Restore or stabilize | Who can stop the clock |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Water moving into a suite, fire-system impairment, a downed power line, a blocked exit | | | |
| P2 | HVAC down in an occupied suite during business hours, a dark parking row at night | | | |
| P3 | A routine repair that can wait for the next vendor day | | | |
| P4 | Planned work already on section 5 or section 8 | | | |

- [ ] Every order has one owner. "The team" is not an owner
- [ ] Closure includes a photo or a note of what "done" means
- [ ] Anything open past your own limit has a written reason

## 7. Vendor control

- [ ] W-9 and entity name match the contract
- [ ] Certificate of insurance on file, landlord as additional insured where you require it
- [ ] License checked where the trade requires one
- [ ] Workers' compensation on file where the law requires it
- [ ] Scope, price, and who may approve extras are in a writing signed before work
- [ ] Certificate end date is on a calendar 30 days ahead, and access stops when the certificate lapses
- [ ] Major contracts (janitorial, landscape, HVAC service, sweeping) have a rebid or a written reason not to rebid on a cycle you chose
- [ ] Invoices are compared with the contract rate before they are paid

## 14. Failures this pack is meant to catch

- A vendor still on site after the certificate expired
- A roof leak described in email and missing from the log
- Capital billed as CAM because the invoice said "repair"
- Two people authorized the same emergency, twice
- The walk skipped the back lot because the front looked fine

## 15. Retail conditions worth a line of their own

- [ ] Food tenants: hood, exhaust, and grease interceptor on a schedule, with the last service date
- [ ] Sign criteria exist, and a new face is checked against them before it is built
- [ ] Anchor or major-tenant facilities contact is a person you have met
- [ ] Tenant build-out: penetrations, exhaust, and sprinklers are reviewed before work, and the drawing on file matches what was built
- [ ] Shared access or drainage that crosses a property line is in the file, not only in someone's head

## The practical next step

1. Fill the after-hours roster before you need it.
2. Walk the common-area list this week and open an order for what you actually see.
3. Put preventive work on dates, not on the word quarterly.
4. Read the repair table against two leases where habit and the lease disagree.
