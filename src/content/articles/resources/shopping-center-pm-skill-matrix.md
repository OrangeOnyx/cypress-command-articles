# Shopping Center PM Skill Matrix

Six competencies, five levels, scored on artifacts. A study frame for a working operator. Work the list below. Progress saves in this browser. [Download the printable blank](/resources/shopping-center-pm-skill-matrix.md).


<div class="ix" data-ix="checklist" data-id="res-skill"></div>

A study frame for a working shopping-center operator. Score observable behavior, not vocabulary. This is operator context for a blank you fill in. It is not legal, tax, or investment advice. Where a lease, a lender, or a statute decides the next step, the worksheet records the question and the document. Counsel answers it.

Credential names below are study paths. Holding one is not the same as operating the center.

*Study framework for sharpening domain judgment across the six core competency areas of retail/shopping-center operations. Built for a working operator, not an exam-taker — every row describes an observable behavior, not a topic to memorize.*

---

## How to Use This Matrix

**The five levels.** Each competency is scored on a five-level progression. Levels are cumulative — a Level 4 operator can still do everything at Level 2.

| Level | Label | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | **Novice** | Executes routine tasks under supervision. Can describe *what* happened but not *why* it matters to NOI. |
| **L2** | **Developing** | Handles standard cases independently. Recognizes when a situation is non-standard and escalates. |
| **L3** | **Proficient PM / GM** | Owns the outcome, not just the task. Explains monthly variance to ownership. Negotiates renewals without hand-holding. This is the "shopping-center General Manager" bar. |
| **L4** | **Strong Operator** | Anticipates. Runs the property on a forward calendar (18–24 months). Reads tenant P&Ls, defends CAM audits, structures blend-and-extends, and preserves NOI through downturns. Portfolio-thinking mindset even at single-asset scale. |
| **L5** | **Expert / Asset-Level** | Investment-fluent. Trades ops decisions against IRR, cap-rate impact, and hold-sell strategy. Sets standards others follow. |

**How to study against it.**
1. Score yourself honestly on each row — no half-levels.
2. Circle the *two* rows where the delta between where you are and where you want to be is largest. Those are your next quarter.
3. For each targeted row, pick one artifact to produce: a completed CAM recon, a signed renewal, a 5-year capex plan, an updated tenant handbook, a hurricane checklist run. Judgment sharpens on artifacts, not reading.
4. Re-score every 90 days.

**Sources.** All row content is compressed from [ICSC CRX](https://www.icsc.com/uploads/t07-subpage/CRX-Global-English.pdf), [ICSC CSM](https://www.icsc.com/uploads/default/CSM_Global_Brief_REcon_2016.pdf), [ICSC CLS](https://www.icsc.com/uploads/t07-subpage/CLS-Global-English-.pdf), [ICSC 2025 CAM Workshop](https://www.icsc.com/uploads/event_documents/2025LC_Workshop_12.pdf), [IREM CPM Handbook 2026](https://www.irem.org/file%20library/globalnavigation/certifications/forindividuals/cpm/cpm-handbook-2026.pdf), [IREM PropTech Insights 2025](https://www.irem.org/file%20library/globalnavigation/learning/tech%20hub/irem-proptech-insights-25-fnl.pdf), [BOMA Foundations](https://boma.org/education/courses/foundations-of-real-estate-management/), [CBRE Global Standards](https://elearnresources.cbre.com/AssetServices/Handbooks/US_Global_Standards_Booklet.pdf), [Retail Green Lease Primer](https://imt.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Retail_Green_Lease_Primer_RILA-IMT22.pdf), and the ICSC operator roundtables on [cost management](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/the-battle-to-control-costs-while-running-successful-centers) and [go-dark clauses](https://www.coxcastle.com/publication-top-10-issues-in-negotiating-go-dark-provisions-in-retail-leases). Local procedure is jurisdiction-specific. Read the statute with counsel; do not treat a citation in a study note as a script.

## Domain 1 — Leasing

*The revenue engine. Every other competency exists to protect the value of what leasing creates.*

| Sub-competency | L1 Novice | L2 Developing | L3 Proficient PM/GM | L4 Strong Operator | L5 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Deal economics** | Focuses on base rent. Reads a lease. | Calculates NER for a straight deal. Understands TI, free rent, LC. | Runs full NER analysis — TI amortized over term, LC discounted, free rent modeled monthly. Presents deal to ownership with 10-yr cash flow. | Compares multiple deal structures side-by-side. Recommends structure based on tenant credit, submarket, hold period, and refi timing. | Trades leasing decisions against cap-rate impact and IRR to owner. Advises on hold-sell timing. |
| **LOI → lease workflow** | Passes docs to attorney. | Drafts a clean LOI from a term sheet. Tracks doc status. | Negotiates LOI independently within owner-approved parameters. Manages the 6–12 week LOI-to-execution cycle. | Anticipates 8–10 negotiation points before they arise. Uses redline history as leverage. Closes atypical deals (large, complex, credit tenant). | Sets deal parameters and delegation limits for the whole team/portfolio. |
| **Key legal terms** | Recognizes base rent, term, use clause. | Explains percentage rent, escalations, TI, exclusives. | Fluent in co-tenancy (opening vs. ongoing), radius, exclusives, kickout, go-dark, recapture, assignment/subletting. | Negotiates go-dark landlord protections (the [Cox Castle 10-point checklist](https://www.coxcastle.com/publication-top-10-issues-in-negotiating-go-dark-provisions-in-retail-leases): continuous-operations covenant, notice, recapture right, cure period, sales-based test, sublease consent, term limit on going-dark, effect on co-tenancy, remedies, waiver). | Drafts custom lease language that has survived multiple portfolio tenants. Anticipates second-order effects of every clause. |
| **Structure selection (NNN / Gross / Mod-Gross)** | Knows the acronyms. | Explains what each shifts to whom. | Selects structure based on tenant type, submarket norm, and admin overhead. Defends NNN to prospects. | Uses structure as a negotiation lever — offers a fixed-CAM alternative to sophisticated tenants to eliminate audit exposure ([ICSC CAM Workshop](https://www.icsc.com/uploads/event_documents/2025LC_Workshop_12.pdf)). | Structures deals to optimize whole-center recovery ratio, not per-deal recovery. |
| **Tenant mix / merchandising** | Knows current tenant roster. | Identifies obvious gaps. | Applies convenience/comparison/destination framework ([ICSC Guide to Improving Tenant Mix](https://www.icsc.com/uploads/gpp/ICSC-Guide-for-Improving-Tenant-Mix.pdf)). Maintains a target-tenant list. | Runs void analysis against trade-area demographics. Uses tenant mix to defend rents and reduce percentage-rent exposure. | Drives repositioning strategy — decides which categories to shed, which anchors to pursue, and manages the multi-year transition. |
| **Prospecting & brokers** | Answers inbound broker calls. | Maintains a broker distribution list. Sends availability blasts. | Runs an outbound prospecting cadence. Cultivates 3–5 broker relationships as preferred partners. | Sources deals directly from national tenants' real-estate committees. Presents at ICSC events. | Sets brokerage strategy, tour incentives, and market presence for the portfolio. |
| **Renewals & restructures** | Sends renewal notice at expiration. | Starts renewal 6 months out. | Starts renewal **12–18 months out**. Presents ownership with retain/replace analysis showing NER, downtime, TI, and LC costs of a new deal. | Runs blend-and-extend as a live playbook — JLL's data shows $110M in tenant savings across 463 restructures during downturn cycles ([JLL restructure insight](https://retail-insider.com/page/271/?m=0)). Uses restructures to lock in credit tenants pre-distress. | Uses restructures across the portfolio to reset weighted-average lease term and defend valuation. |
| **Negotiation posture** | Concedes to close. | Holds firm on obvious asks. | Uses BATNA framing. Documents concessions with a value swap. | Trades economic and non-economic terms deliberately (e.g., extra free rent in exchange for a personal guaranty, or narrowed exclusive). | Negotiates multi-party deals (co-anchors, JVs, master leases, ground leases). |

## Domain 2 — Tenant Relations

*The retention layer. Every renewal you save is a leasing commission and TI package you don't spend.*

| Sub-competency | L1 Novice | L2 Developing | L3 Proficient PM/GM | L4 Strong Operator | L5 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Onboarding / move-in** | Hands over keys. | Runs a move-in checklist (CO, utility transfer, COI, signage permit). | Owns a full opening kit: tenant handbook, emergency contacts, marketing kit, portal access, sales-reporting instructions. | Onboarding is a scored process — tenant NPS captured at day 30/90. Feeds insights back into the handbook. | Portfolio-wide onboarding standard others adopt. |
| **Site-visit cadence** | Reactive — visits when there's a complaint. | Monthly walk-through, informal. | Structured monthly visits with documented tenant-relations log per [CBRE Global Standards](https://elearnresources.cbre.com/AssetServices/Handbooks/US_Global_Standards_Booklet.pdf). Talks to every tenant quarterly. | Reads store health from the walk — inventory depth, staff engagement, promotional signage, foot traffic — before tenant tells you anything. | Trains other managers on how to read a store. |
| **Sales & business-health monitoring** | Doesn't collect sales. | Collects when the lease requires it. Files it. | Tracks occupancy cost ratio (OCR = total occupancy cost ÷ sales) by tenant. Flags any tenant >15% ([ICSC CRX D4.5.4](https://www.icsc.com/uploads/t07-subpage/CRX-Global-English.pdf)). | Reads tenant P&L and four-wall economics. Identifies distress **before** delinquency — inventory turn slowdown, staff cuts, marketing pullback ([ICSC CSM D5](https://www.icsc.com/uploads/default/CSM_Global_Brief_REcon_2016.pdf)). | Uses OCR trends across categories to reset target rents and re-tenant weak categories proactively. |
| **Delinquency workflow (Louisiana-specific)** | Emails a reminder. | Sends late-fee notice per lease. | Runs the lease's own sequence: courtesy, the late fee the lease states, written default, and cure. When cure ends, hands the file to counsel for any possession filing. Does not invent a day-count from another property's form. | Avoids the acceptance-of-rent trap. Uses Waiver of Notice provisions cleanly. Never attempts self-help lockout. Coordinates local counsel efficiently. | Uses delinquency data to inform underwriting of future deals and reserve requirements. |
| **Distressed tenants** | Escalates to attorney. | Requests financials. | Structures rent-deferral or forbearance agreements with cure schedules. | Runs a full workout playbook: rent deferral, term extension for concession, partial recapture, right-sizing, relocation, or lease buyout — chooses based on tenant credit, store performance, and re-leasing risk. | Decides workout vs. terminate based on portfolio-level occupancy strategy and asset-level valuation impact. |
| **Bankruptcy handling** | "Call the lawyer." | Understands 11 USC §365 assume/reject exists. | Knows the 120/210-day deadline under §365(d)(4). Files timely claims. Knows the §502(b)(6) landlord cap on damages. | Applies §365(b)(3) shopping-center protections in stipulations: preserves tenant mix, use clauses, and radius restrictions through assumption. | Advises ownership on portfolio-wide bankruptcy exposure and reserve strategy. |
| **Co-tenancy triggers** | Doesn't know what co-tenancy is. | Recognizes clause exists. | Distinguishes opening (pre-open threshold) vs. ongoing (post-open remedy). Tracks anchor status against co-tenancy list. | Runs co-tenancy stress test annually: which tenants can invoke rent relief or termination if which anchor leaves? Pre-positions replacement anchors. | Structures new leases to minimize co-tenancy chain reactions. |
| **Specialty leasing / short-term** | None. | Sells occasional kiosk. | Runs licensed short-term program (RMU/kiosk/pop-up) — license, not lease — with clear termination and standards ([ICSC CSM D7](https://www.icsc.com/uploads/default/CSM_Global_Brief_REcon_2016.pdf)). | Uses specialty leasing as incubation pipeline into long-term deals. Extracts sponsorship revenue from common areas. | Builds specialty leasing into an operating line generating material % of NOI. |
| **Community & marketing events** | None. | Ad hoc. | Runs a marketing fund (per lease). Delivers seasonal events tied to tenant sales windows. | Ties events to tenant sales lift and reports ROI. Uses events as tenant-retention lever. | Runs a branded property program that drives measurable trade-area capture. |

## Domain 3 — CAM & Operating Expense Administration

*The recovery engine. Where most single-asset operators leak the most money.*

| Sub-competency | L1 Novice | L2 Developing | L3 Proficient PM/GM | L4 Strong Operator | L5 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What CAM includes / excludes** | Reads the CAM clause. | Categorizes standard opex against the lease. | Distinguishes controllable vs. non-controllable. Excludes capex, financing, marketing, and specifically excluded items per lease. | Maintains a per-lease CAM inclusion/exclusion matrix (leases rarely match). Codes every invoice to the recoverable line real-time — not at year-end. | Structures new lease CAM language to maximize recoverability without triggering audit disputes. |
| **Pro-rata share** | Uses stated %. | Calculates tenant SF ÷ total GLA. | Verifies GLA denominator: excludes anchor-owned parcels, third-party outlots, and non-CAM areas ([ICSC 2025 CAM Workshop](https://www.icsc.com/uploads/event_documents/2025LC_Workshop_12.pdf)). | Negotiates minimum GFA clauses so redevelopment doesn't dilute the pool. Reconciles denominator changes annually. | Aligns denominator methodology across the portfolio for defensibility. |
| **Caps & floors** | Doesn't know what a cap is. | Recognizes 5% cap language. | Distinguishes cumulative vs. non-cumulative, compounding vs. simple. Applies correctly at reconciliation. | Understands that a cumulative-and-compounding cap frequently recovers *more* over term than a non-cumulative cap and pushes for it in negotiations. Excludes non-controllables from caps. | Structures cap language across the portfolio to hit target recovery ratio. |
| **Gross-ups** | Doesn't gross up. | Grosses up total opex. | Grosses up **only variable expenses** to 95% occupancy (or lease-specified threshold). Documents methodology. | Never grosses up fixed costs (insurance, taxes, security, landscape). Defends methodology in audit. | Trains team; standardizes across portfolio. |
| **Admin fees** | Not applied. | Applied per lease. | Distinguishes admin fee (% of CAM) from mgmt fee (% of revenue). Applies to CAM base only, not to insurance or taxes if lease excludes. | Negotiates 15% admin fees on CAM and defends against tenant push to 10% or 12%. | Structures fee language for portfolio consistency. |
| **Annual reconciliation** | Sends year-end statement. | Runs the 5-step process: budget close → actuals compile → recon calc → notice to tenant → true-up billing/credit. | Executes recon within 120 days of year-end. Provides tenants with backup on request. Resolves disputes in writing. | Anticipates the 3–5 tenants likely to challenge; pre-builds audit-ready backup for those. Uses recon as a relationship-building moment, not a fight. | Uses recon data to inform next-year budgeting and lease-negotiation strategy portfolio-wide. |
| **Estoppel / audit response** | Panics. | Provides basic backup. | Handles tenant audits professionally: scopes audit rights per lease, provides only required backup, resolves findings without opening unrelated years. | Preemptively "audits self" quarterly. Maintains complete, organized records per year. Never triggers an audit through sloppy billing. | Turns audits into a positive tenant experience that strengthens renewal. |
| **Recoveries strategy** | Bills what the software calculates. | Reviews recovery ratio annually. | Targets 92–98% recovery ratio. Reviews all opex line-by-line for recoverability. Appeals property tax assessments where economics support. | Real-time expense coding, capex-vs-repair judgment on every $10K+ invoice, energy procurement in off-peak months ([Karen Raquet, JLL, ICSC roundtable](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/the-battle-to-control-costs-while-running-successful-centers)). | Sets portfolio-wide recovery targets and holds team accountable. |

## Domain 4 — Maintenance & Vendor Coordination

*The asset preservation engine. Deferred maintenance is a silent loan against future NOI.*

| Sub-competency | L1 Novice | L2 Developing | L3 Proficient PM/GM | L4 Strong Operator | L5 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Preventive maintenance program** | Runs to failure. | Has an HVAC service contract. | Maintains a full PM calendar: roof inspections (2×/yr Gulf Coast), HVAC/RTU quarterly, parking lot sealcoat every 3–5 yrs / overlay 12–15 yrs, striping annually, landscape weekly, photocell/lighting monthly, fire alarm annual test, sprinkler quarterly, backflow annual, pylon annual. | PM plan is calendar-driven and budget-integrated. Every item has a useful-life expectancy and a replacement year on the 10-year capex plan. Adjusts frequency by Gulf Coast climate (humidity, hurricane, salt). | Sets PM standards across portfolio. Uses IoT/predictive maintenance where payback exists ([IREM PropTech Insights 2025](https://www.irem.org/file%20library/globalnavigation/learning/tech%20hub/irem-proptech-insights-25-fnl.pdf) — 46.3% AI adoption for BMS). |
| **Work order intake & SLAs** | Ad hoc email/text. | Uses a work-order system. | Runs P1–P4 priority framework: P1 = 15-min ack / 2-hr resolve (life safety, water intrusion); P2 = 4-hr / 24-hr (HVAC down in an occupied unit); P3 = 48-hr / 7-day (routine); P4 = planned. Escalation path documented. | Tracks SLA compliance monthly. Reports to ownership. Adjusts vendor rosters based on SLA performance. | Sets portfolio SLAs and integrates with tenant-satisfaction KPIs. |
| **Vendor onboarding** | Uses whoever's cheapest. | Collects W-9 and COI. | Full onboarding: Louisiana LSLBC license verification, COI with landlord as additional insured, W-9, MSA, background check, references. Reviews COI expiration quarterly. | Runs a scored vendor performance program: pricing, quality, SLA compliance, safety record. Reviews annually. | Manages preferred-vendor program across portfolio; leverages scale for pricing. |
| **Bidding & procurement** | Auto-renews. | Occasional rebid. | Rebids major contracts (janitorial, security, landscape, HVAC PM) on a 2–3 year cycle. Uses 3-bid rule minimum. | Demands itemized cost-justification for any YoY increase; challenges utility bills line-by-line; locks energy contracts in off-peak months ([ICSC operator roundtable](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/the-battle-to-control-costs-while-running-successful-centers)). Right-sizes service frequency to data (footfall, cameras). | Negotiates portfolio-level national contracts. |
| **CapEx planning** | Reactive. | Annual capex budget. | Maintains a rolling 5-year capex plan using EUL tables (roof 20–25 yr TPO, HVAC 15–20 yr, parking overlay 12–15 yr, striping 2–3 yr). Funds reserves at 2–4% of CRV. | Runs a reserve study every 3–5 years. Amortizes capital recoveries per lease (never billed lump-sum). Evaluates every capex against payback and NOI impact. | Integrates capex plan with hold-sell strategy. Recommends capex timing to optimize disposition value. |
| **Emergency response** | Calls someone. | Has a call tree. | Runs hurricane 72/48/24-hr protocols. Water intrusion, break-in, and fire response scripts written. Local government, utility, and law-enforcement contacts on file for the jurisdiction you actually operate in. | Tests protocols annually. Coordinates with tenants pre-storm. Maintains post-storm damage assessment templates and NFIP 60-day Proof of Loss timeline. | Sets portfolio emergency-response standard. |
| **Landlord vs. tenant obligation triage** | Argues with tenant. | Checks the lease. | Runs a triage table by NNN standard: roof/structure/parking lot = LL; HVAC unit serving demised premises = T (typically); water intrusion origin drives allocation. Documents every judgment call in writing. | Anticipates the 3–5 recurring dispute points at the property and pre-clarifies with tenants at onboarding. | Standardizes lease language across portfolio to eliminate ambiguity. |

## Domain 5 — Reporting & Financial Operations

*The credibility engine. Ownership trusts the operator who explains variance before being asked.*

| Sub-competency | L1 Novice | L2 Developing | L3 Proficient PM/GM | L4 Strong Operator | L5 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Monthly operating report** | Runs the report from software. | Delivers on time with rent roll and delinquency. | Delivers by working day 10: cover memo, financial statement, budget vs. actual with variance explanations, rent roll, aged AR, leasing activity, capex status, occupancy, maintenance highlights. | Variance analysis is proactive: every line >±5% or ±$5K is explained with a corrective action. Cover memo tells ownership the *story* of the month. | Sets reporting standard for team. Uses monthly package as a dashboard for asset-management decisions. |
| **Rent roll accuracy** | Prints from software. | Reconciles monthly against leases. | Rent roll ties exactly to lease commencements, escalations, and CAM billings. Zero variance to GL. | Maintains a lease-abstraction discipline: every executed lease abstracted into the system within 48 hours. Zero abstraction errors caught by tenants. | Standardizes abstraction protocol across portfolio. |
| **Budget build** | Copies last year + 3%. | Builds line-by-line from prior actuals. | Runs zero-based budget on controllables. Escalates fixed contracts per contractual terms. Builds recoveries from budget, not from a fixed % assumption. | Presents multiple scenarios (base, upside, downside). Tests NOI sensitivity to a 5% occupancy drop, a major tenant loss, and a 20% utility spike. Links budget to leasing pipeline (SNO). | Ties budget to underwriting model and lender covenants. Advises ownership on capex-vs.-hold decisions. |
| **NOI, cap rate, valuation impact** | Doesn't compute. | Knows the formula. | Explains how a $10K opex reduction at 7% cap = $143K in value. Uses that math in every operational decision. | Runs value-creation levers deliberately: releasing at market, lease-up, opex reduction, capex-driven NOI lift, recovery-ratio improvement. Quantifies each in dollar and cap-rate impact. | Ties operational plan to disposition timing and IRR. |
| **KPIs tracked** | Occupancy only. | Occupancy, delinquency, retention. | 12-KPI dashboard: occupancy %, sales PSF (where reported), OCR by tenant, retention %, delinquency %, NOI growth, TI PSF, LC PSF, rent spread on new/renewal, SNO pipeline, recovery ratio, capex vs. plan. | Benchmarks against public REIT reporting: Kimco (96.4% occupancy, +3.0% same-property NOI), Regency (98%, +26% rent spread), Kite (+2.9% NOI). Shares monthly vs. quarterly cadence appropriately. | Sets KPI targets and cascades across team. |
| **Reforecasting** | Doesn't reforecast. | Annual reforecast at midyear. | Reforecasts quarterly with rolling 12-month view. Adjusts for material leasing events, capex slippage, expense surprises. | Runs a live reforecast — updates within 30 days of any material event (major tenant default, insurance renewal, tax assessment change). | Aligns reforecast cadence with owner/lender reporting requirements. |
| **Lender & owner reporting** | Sends what's asked. | Meets covenant reporting deadlines. | Reports proactively on covenant status: DSCR, debt yield, occupancy, loan-agreement compliance. | Anticipates covenant-tripping events 2 quarters in advance. Advises ownership on refi timing. | Manages lender relationships at portfolio level. |

## Domain 6 — Compliance, Risk & Legal

*The insurance policy against every other domain going right.*

| Sub-competency | L1 Novice | L2 Developing | L3 Proficient PM/GM | L4 Strong Operator | L5 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Insurance program** | Renews annually. | Reviews policy at renewal. | Maintains the stack the center actually needs: landlord general liability, umbrella, property, business interruption, equipment breakdown, and flood where the zone or the lender requires it. Enforces tenant minimums per ICSC guidance: $1M/$2M CGL, $5M umbrella, $3M F&B liquor liability. | Runs an annual insurance review with broker: benchmarks premiums, adjusts limits, closes gaps (cyber, terrorism, ordinance-and-law). Tracks certificate compliance real-time. | Structures insurance program across portfolio. Uses loss data to inform premium negotiations. |
| **ADA (Title III)** | Doesn't know. | Recognizes parking count requirements. | Maintains ADA compliance: van-accessible ratios, accessible route from parking to entrance, 60% of primary entrances accessible, accessible restrooms. Documents last review. | Runs a periodic ADA audit. Understands "readily achievable" barrier removal standard. Coordinates ADA-compliant TI work at every new deal. | Portfolio ADA program with periodic third-party audits. |
| **OSHA basics** | Doesn't apply to me. | Knows General Duty Clause. | Applies HazCom/GHS for stored chemicals, LOTO for maintenance, fall protection for roof access. Requires contractor OSHA compliance in MSA. | Runs safety audits. Tracks incident data. Trains vendors on site-specific safety. | Sets safety program across portfolio; targets zero recordables. |
| **Local permits & regulations** | Renews when reminded. | Tracks CO, occupancy permits. | Owns the local permit map: building official, health department for food uses, and alcohol licensing where a tenant sells it. Every tenant permit tracked. Names change by parish. | Anticipates ordinance changes. Sits on local boards. Uses relationships to expedite tenant openings. | Portfolio-wide regulatory monitoring; advocates for favorable ordinances. |
| **Environmental** | Doesn't think about it. | Knows Phase I ASTM E1527. | Runs Phase I at acquisition and per lender requirement. Maintains underground storage tank registration and closure records with the state environmental agency. Enforces grease-trap compliance for F&B. Tracks CAA §608 refrigerant handling. | Runs environmental risk as an ongoing program: tenant use monitoring, grease/hazmat inspections, storage inventories, spill kits, incident logs. | Portfolio environmental program integrated with capex and disposition due diligence. |
| **Data / tenant PII** | Not considered. | Basic password hygiene. | Applies Louisiana Breach Notification Law (La. R.S. 51:3071). PCI-DSS compliance for any card processing. Access controls on tenant portal. | Runs a data map: what PII is collected, where stored, who has access, what retention rules apply. Incident-response playbook written. Vendor security requirements enforced. | Portfolio-wide data-governance program. |
| **Louisiana eviction & lease enforcement** | "Call the lawyer." | Sends a demand letter. | Knows the local possession path well enough to brief counsel and to avoid waiving notice. Never uses self-help. Does not file, and does not tell anyone else to file, from this matrix. | Structures notices to avoid the acceptance-of-rent trap. Coordinates efficiently with local counsel — legal is the last resort, not the first. Documents everything. | Advises other operators. Uses enforcement data to inform underwriting. |
| **Hurricane / emergency preparedness** | Nailed plywood last time. | Has a call tree. | Runs Gulf Coast protocol: pre-season checklist, 72/48/24-hr checklists, post-storm damage assessment, NFIP 60-day Proof of Loss deadline. Tenant communication scripts pre-drafted. | Tests protocols annually. Coordinates with tenants pre-storm. Has vendors on standby with pre-signed MSAs. Insurance broker pre-briefed. | Sets Gulf Coast emergency standard for portfolio. |

## Cross-Cutting Meta-Competencies

*These aren't in any exam outline but separate strong operators from title-holders.*

| Meta-competency | L1 | L2 | L3 | L4 | L5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Owner communication** | Reports when asked. | On-time monthly package. | Delivers the story, not the data. Proactively surfaces risk. | Ownership calls *you* before making decisions. | Trusted advisor across ownership entities. |
| **Time horizon** | This week. | This quarter. | Rolling 12 months. | 18–24 month forward calendar; every action is one link in a chain. | Multi-year strategic plan tied to hold-sell. |
| **Tenant partnership mindset** | Tenant = payer. | Tenant = customer. | Tenant success = my success ([JLL SVP framing](https://retail-insider.com/page/271/?m=0)): if they do the sales, I can raise rent. | Structures deals and operations to accelerate tenant success, especially in weak categories. | Uses tenant-success data as a leasing pitch to new prospects. |
| **Data & PropTech fluency** | Software = rent roll only. | Uses work-order system. | Uses BI dashboards for KPIs. Reads footfall/sales/OCR together. | Deploys AI/ML where payback exists — AP automation, lease abstraction, predictive maintenance ([IREM PropTech 2025](https://www.irem.org/file%20library/globalnavigation/learning/tech%20hub/irem-proptech-insights-25-fnl.pdf)). Builds custom tooling where off-the-shelf falls short. | Sets tech strategy for portfolio; evaluates PropTech investments against NOI impact. |
| **Sustainability / green lease** | Not on the radar. | LED where obvious. | Runs energy tracking. ## Recommended Study Path

**Score honestly, then focus.** Most working single-asset operators land at L2–L3 across most domains, with pockets of L4 in areas they've been forced into (usually maintenance and leasing) and pockets of L1 in areas that "haven't come up yet" (usually CAM audits, bankruptcy, and environmental).

**High-leverage sequence:**

1. **CAM administration (Domain 3)** — most single-asset operators leak 3–8% of recoverable expense here. One clean reconciliation lifts you from L2 to L3 permanently. Study: [ICSC 2025 CAM Workshop PDF](https://www.icsc.com/uploads/event_documents/2025LC_Workshop_12.pdf).

2. **Deal economics (Domain 1)** — the ability to compute NER and present a retain/replace analysis to ownership separates the L3 GM from the L2 assistant. Build a reusable NER spreadsheet. Study: [ICSC CLS Domain 2 & CRX Domain 4](https://www.icsc.com/uploads/t07-subpage/CLS-Global-English-.pdf).

3. **Delinquency and possession (Domain 6)** — local mastery matters, and it belongs with counsel. Read the notice and cure clauses in your own leases, then walk a mock file with counsel. Do not practice a filing from this matrix.

4. **CapEx plan (Domain 4)** — build a 5-year capex plan with EUL-based replacement years for every major system at the center you operate. Reserve at 2–4% of CRV. This artifact upgrades multiple rows at once.

5. **Financial reporting & KPIs (Domain 5)** — adopt the 12-KPI dashboard. Benchmark quarterly against Kimco / Regency / Kite disclosures.

**Credentials to pursue** (as recognized proof of the underlying competency, not as substitute for it):

- **[IREM ACoM](https://www.irem.org/certifications/for-individuals/acom-accredited-commercial-manager/accredited-commercial-manager-acom-step-1-learn)** — 12-month path, hits L3 CAM, budgeting, leasing basics, risk. Best entry-to-mid credential.
- **[IREM CPM](https://www.irem.org/file%20library/globalnavigation/certifications/forindividuals/cpm/cpm-handbook-2026.pdf)** — 36-month experience path, 8 courses + Capstone. Deepest investment-analysis training available. This is the L4→L5 credential.
- **[BOMA Foundations of Real Estate Management](https://boma.org/education/courses/foundations-of-real-estate-management/)** — 30-hour intro, good scaffolding for anyone bootstrapping into the industry.

*ICSC credentials (CRX, CSM, CLS) are no longer testable but their exam specifications remain the [best-documented shopping-center competency framework in the industry](https://www.icsc.com/attend-and-learn/professional-development/education/existing-credential-designations) — use the PDFs above as reading lists, not as certification paths.*

2. Circle two rows. Those are the quarter.
3. Produce one artifact for each circled row: a reconciliation, a renewal memo, a capital list, or a report.
4. Rescore in 90 days against the artifact, not against how the quarter felt.
