**Natural Breakpoint Formula:**

```
Natural Breakpoint = Annual Base Rent ÷ Percentage Rent Rate

Example: $120,000 base rent ÷ 6% = $2,000,000 natural breakpoint
```

## Part 3 — Gross Sales Definition Checklist

The lease's gross sales definition controls what is included and excluded. Work through this checklist before calculating percentage rent.

### Inclusions (confirm these ARE counted)

- [ ] All cash and credit card sales from the leased premises
- [ ] Online orders fulfilled from or picked up at the premises (if lease covers omnichannel)
- [ ] Gift card redemptions (at time of redemption, not issuance)
- [ ] Layaway payments (at time of completion)
- [ ] Delivery charges billed to customers
- [ ] Sales by sublicensees or concessionaires operating within the space

### Standard Exclusions (confirm these are NOT counted)

- [ ] Sales tax collected and remitted to government
- [ ] Returns and allowances (net of restocking fees)
- [ ] Employee discounts (up to lease cap, typically 5–10% of sales)
- [ ] Gift card issuances (not redemptions)
- [ ] Transfers between tenant locations (inter-store transfers)
- [ ] Sales of fixtures, equipment, or assets not in ordinary course
- [ ] Insurance proceeds
- [ ] Bad debt write-offs (if lease permits)

## Part 5 — Three-Year Audit Comparison

Track percentage rent trends across three audit periods to identify anomalies.

| Metric | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | 3-Yr Trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reported Gross Sales | $ | $ | $ | |
| Adjusted Gross Sales | $ | $ | $ | |
| Breakpoint | $ | $ | $ | |
| Sales Above Breakpoint | $ | $ | $ | |
| Percentage Rent Owed | $ | $ | $ | |
| Percentage Rent Paid | $ | $ | $ | |
| True-Up Amount | $ | $ | $ | |
| YoY Sales Growth | — | % | % | |
| Occupancy Cost Ratio (OCR) | % | % | % | |

**OCR Formula:**
```
OCR = (Base Rent + Percentage Rent + CAM) ÷ Gross Sales

Healthy range: 8–12% for inline tenants
Anchor tenants: 4–7%
Food & Beverage: 10–15%
```

**Red Flags in the Three-Year Comparison:**
- Sales decline >10% YoY without documented external cause (store closure, renovation)
- OCR consistently below 6% for inline tenants (may indicate underreporting)
- Sales plateau within 2–5% of breakpoint for 2+ consecutive years (artificial suppression pattern)
- Sudden large exclusion claims not present in prior years

## Part 6 — Audit Rights Trigger Analysis

If variance exceeds the lease threshold, the landlord has the right to demand a formal audit at the tenant's expense (if the audit reveals underpayment above the threshold).

| Trigger Condition | Threshold | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Variance between reported and verified sales | ___% | ☐ Triggered  ☐ Clear |
| Sales decline >___% YoY without cause | ___% | ☐ Triggered  ☐ Clear |
| Missing or late sales reports | Per lease | ☐ Triggered  ☐ Clear |
| Refusal to provide supporting documentation | Per lease | ☐ Triggered  ☐ Clear |

**If audit rights are triggered:**

1. Calendar the audit-rights window in the lease (often one to three years from the report). Have counsel review any notice before it is sent
2. If counsel agrees an audit is available, engage a CPA or retail auditor
3. Ask for the support the lease allows: point-of-sale reports, bank statements, sales-tax returns, card-processor summaries
4. Write the findings down before anyone drafts a demand. Part 7 is the handoff, not the letter
