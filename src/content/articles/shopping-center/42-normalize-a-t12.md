# How to Normalize a T12 Statement for CRE Underwriting

A trailing twelve-month operating statement is the property's recent income and expense, month by month, usually prepared by the owner. Private deals rarely come with an audit. The T12 is rich and untidy. Normalization is the work of turning that owner view into a stabilized net operating income a buyer or lender can trace. "Reading a Shopping Center P&L" walks the lines. This article is the adjustment file that sits on top of them, and the rules a model may apply only after a person has written them down.

Every amount in the bridge is hypothetical. It is not On The Blvd and not any listing.

## What the T12 is, and what it is not

The raw T12 shows how this owner ran the property. Stabilized NOI is a different statement: market occupancy, a market management fee, recurring expenses, capital items taken out of repairs, and one-time income removed. T12 NOI is the start. Stabilized NOI is the underwriting basis. A model that treats them as the same number has not done the job.

| Component | What the owner line shows | The normalization question |
|---|---|---|
| Gross potential rent | Scheduled rent if full | Does it match the current rent roll? |
| Vacancy and credit loss | What was empty or uncollected | Is that rate sustainable, or a lucky year? |
| Other income | Recoveries, parking, signage, misc. | Which items will not happen again? |
| Operating expenses | What was paid | What is below market, personal, or capital? |
| NOI | Income minus expenses | The output, after the bridge |

Most lenders will not treat a short history as a T12. If you have fewer than twelve months, say so. Annualizing from fewer than about ten months of operations is a limitation to write in the file, not a trick to hide.

## Income

Reconcile gross potential rent to the rent roll before you touch expenses. If the roll is higher, recent renewals may not be in the trailing months yet. If the T12 is higher, tenants may have left or rents may have been cut. Either gap is a sentence in the memo.

Vacancy on the T12 is actual. A stabilized retail screen often used in teaching files is 5 to 10 percent vacancy and credit loss. If the trailing year shows 2 percent, the underwriter has to decide whether that holds. The model may compute both. It may not pick the rate.

Remove non-recurring income from stabilized NOI, and mention it in the narrative so the vacancy it left behind is not a mystery:

- Lease termination fees
- Insurance proceeds
- One-time tenant settlements
- Condemnation awards
- Catch-up deferred rent that will not repeat
- Grants or other one-time outside money

A termination fee is not stabilized income. The suite it vacated still needs a lease-up story: downtime, tenant improvements, and commissions, as their own lines.

## Expenses

Management fee is the most common inflation of owner NOI. Owners who manage the property themselves often charge nothing, or charge a related company a token fee. A retail screen used in these drafts is 4 to 6 percent of effective gross income, regardless of what was paid. The rate is the owner's assumption, written down. The model applies it and cites the line it replaced.

Capital items — a roof, a parking-lot resurfacing, a major HVAC replacement, a large tenant improvement — often sit in repairs. Pull them out of operating expenses. Put a reserve back. A retail teaching range is $0.15 to $0.50 per square foot per year. That range is not a quote and not a reserve for a named center.

| Expense | Action | A screen to write down, then check |
|---|---|---|
| Management fee | Normalize if missing or thin | 4–6 percent of EGI for many retail files |
| Capital items | Remove from operations; reserve instead | $0.15–$0.50/SF per year is a teaching band |
| Property tax | Tie to the bill | Model reassessment on a sale; use the local millage |
| Insurance | Tie to the declarations page | Confirm the coverage is the coverage you will carry |
| Repairs | Separate one-time from recurring | $0.50–$1.50/SF per year is a teaching band for retail |
| Utilities | Split landlord-paid from tenant-paid | Follow the leases |
| TI and leasing commissions | Add a reserve if rollover is real | $5–$15/SF on the area you expect to turn is a teaching band |

Taxes and insurance are not "normalized to a national average." They are verified, then adjusted only for a change you can explain, such as a reassessment at the purchase price.

## Add-backs

Add-backs are costs in the T12 that are not costs of this property. They hide under ordinary labels: professional fees, administrative, vehicle. The model can flag a vehicle line. A person needs the invoice or the tax return before the add-back hits NOI.

Common shapes:

- Personal auto charged to the property
- Payroll for a family member with no property job
- Personal legal or accounting
- Life insurance or an unrelated policy
- Personal travel
- A related-party management fee above the market rate you adopted

If you cannot tie the add-back, leave it in expenses and say so. An unsupported add-back is how a file overstates NOI.

## An illustrative bridge

Hypothetical center, hypothetical books. Signs show the direction of the adjustment to NOI. Parentheses reduce stabilized NOI.

| Line | Amount | Source in the teaching file |
|---|---|---|
| T12 NOI, as reported | $485,000 | Owner T12, page 1 |
| Management fee normalized to 5 percent of EGI | ($28,400) | Fee was missing or thin |
| Vacancy set to 7 percent from 2 percent actual | ($18,200) | Assumption written by the reviewer |
| Roof repair moved out of operations | $42,000 | Invoice; reserve is a separate line, not this add-back |
| Owner auto and family payroll added back | $14,500 | Invoices |
| Termination fee removed | ($25,000) | Other income; one time |
| Stabilized NOI | $469,900 | Underwriting basis |

The arithmetic: 485,000 − 28,400 − 18,200 + 42,000 + 14,500 − 25,000 = 469,900. The roof move raises NOI because the cash cost leaves operations. The reserve that replaces it belongs in the cash-flow page, or the bridge is only half done. A model may fill this table. It may not choose 5 percent or 7 percent. Those rates are in the assumption log.

Tie the T12 to Schedule E or Form 8825 when you have the return. A large income gap between the operating statement and the return is a question, not a footnote the model may smooth.

## Where a model helps, and the four rules

A model can read a PDF, a workbook, or a scan; map lines to a chart of accounts; apply rules you wrote; and attach a citation. It should flag lines it cannot classify. The person reviews flags, overrides in a log, and signs the NOI.

Four rules, the same ones the governance guide requires:

1. No extraction starts until the packet is listed, including the months covered.
2. Every adjustment cites a document and a page or cell.
3. The credit or purchase decision names a human author.
4. An override records the name, the time, and the reason.

Confidence theater is not a rule. If a page is unreadable, the line is blank until someone reads it.

## Errors that move NOI the wrong way

| Error | What it does to NOI | How it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| Capital left in repairs | Too low | Any repair line large enough to be a project; ask for the invoice |
| No management fee | Too high | The fee line is blank on an owner-managed property |
| Trailing vacancy used as stabilized when it is unusually low | Too high | Compare the year to a stated market rate |
| One-time income kept | Too high | Other income that does not repeat on the rent roll |
| Several entities in one statement | Unclear | Ask for the property P&L and tie it to the return |
| A short year treated as a full year | Either way | Count the months; do not annualize a partial year quietly |
| Tax held at the current assessment | Often too low after a sale | Model the reassessment with the local rate |

### The view from Arnould Blvd

On The Blvd, 101–149 Arnould Blvd, Lafayette, is roughly 63,000 square feet, 27 units, two buildings. Command Platform is the lease record. This article does not publish a T12, a tax bill, or an NOI for that center. The plate is the habit. An owner-prepared year will omit a management fee the owner never charged themselves, and it will not show a recorded constraint. Stabilized NOI without the fee is a fiction. A model that copies the owner's NOI into a lender package has automated the fiction. The reviewer writes the fee assumption, cites the blank line, and signs.

## The practical next step

1. Mark the months on the T12. If they are not twelve, stop and label the file partial.
2. Tie gross potential rent to the rent roll in one sentence: higher, lower, or matched, and why.
3. Copy the bridge, replace the teaching numbers with the deal, and leave a blank source cell rather than a guessed one.
4. List add-backs you cannot support. They stay in expenses.
5. Put the assumption log (management fee rate, vacancy rate, reserve) on the same page as the NOI, with a name.
