# Rent Roll Analysis for Shopping Centers: How to Read, Stress-Test, and Act on It

A rent roll is a snapshot: who occupies the center, on what terms, as of a date. Lenders size debt from it. Buyers price from it. Operators plan expirations from it. The shorter series piece, "Reading a Rent Roll: What the Numbers Actually Tell You," decodes the columns and four ratios. This practitioner reference is the stress test: physical occupancy versus economic occupancy, a rollover schedule, in-place rent versus a rent you could defend, recovery structure, and credit that is more than a logo.

Every tenant, suite, and dollar in this article is invented. The center is **Cedar Crossing**, an illustrative 75,000-square-foot open-air property. It is not a real shopping center. Harbor Grocery, Northline Pharmacy, Sample Goods, Lantern Cafe, Maple Dental, Cedar Fitness, Pine Outfitters, and Birch Home are not real tenants. Do not treat the ratios as a market study. They exist so the arithmetic can be checked.

The roll is not the leases, and it is not collections. Contract rent is what the paper says is owed. A delinquency report is what arrived. Read both.

## What a usable roll contains

At minimum: tenant, suite, area, commencement, expiration, base rent per square foot, annual base rent, recovery structure, options, and the clauses that change income if someone leaves (co-tenancy, exclusive use, termination). Sales, guaranty, and deposit columns earn their place when percentage rent or credit is part of the story.

Anchors — a grocer, a larger box — often occupy a large share of area and a smaller share of rent. Their value in the model is traffic, and their lease controls more of the inline income than their own rent line suggests. Inline tenants pay more per foot and depend on that traffic. Read the anchor's term, options, and co-tenancy before you celebrate the inline rates.

## Columns, and the flag in each one

| Column | Tie it to | Flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Area | Survey and the lease exhibit | Roll and lease disagree |
| Commencement | The executed lease | Blank or inconsistent |
| Expiration | The lease, not a holdover assumption | Inside twelve months of closing |
| Base rent | The lease and any steps | Far from a rent you can support, with a long term left |
| Recovery | Gross, modified gross, or net | Gross, so you keep the expenses |
| Options | Count, notice, rent method | A below-market formula already written |
| Guaranty | Who signed, and for how long | A local tenant with none |
| Deposit | Amount and the return conditions | A new or thin tenant with none |

If the formula and the signed rent disagree by a rounding difference, the signed rent wins. Footnote it. Do not "fix" an executed deal in the model.

## Cedar Crossing, as of a hypothetical close

Occupied area is 67,000 square feet. Vacant area is 8,000. Physical occupancy is 67,000 ÷ 75,000 = 89.3%.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Harbor Grocery | A-1 | 30,000 | $11.00 | $330,000 | 2032 | NNN, excluded from the CAM denominator |
| Northline Pharmacy | A-2 | 9,000 | $20.00 | $180,000 | 2031 | NNN |
| Sample Goods | B-1 | 4,500 | $26.00 | $117,000 | 2029 | NNN |
| Lantern Cafe | B-2 | 2,400 | $35.00 | $84,000 | 2028 | NNN, plus percentage rent |
| Maple Dental | B-3 | 1,800 | $24.00 | $43,200 | 2027 | Modified gross |
| Cedar Fitness | C-1 | 7,500 | $16.00 | $120,000 | 2029 | NNN |
| Pine Outfitters | C-2 | 6,200 | $22.00 | $136,400 | 2030 | NNN |
| Birch Home | C-3 | 5,600 | $18.00 | $100,800 | 2027 | NNN |
| Vacant | D-1 | 8,000 | — | — | — | — |

Annual base rent in occupancy is $1,111,400. Check the sum before you trust any ratio built on it: 330,000 + 180,000 + 117,000 + 84,000 + 43,200 + 120,000 + 136,400 + 100,800.

Harbor Grocery is 30,000 ÷ 75,000 = 40% of area and 330,000 ÷ 1,111,400 = 29.7% of in-place base rent. Inline tenants carry the rest. That is a normal shape. It is also anchor dependency if inline leases give a remedy when the grocer goes dark. List those leases before you call the roll diversified.

## Physical occupancy is not economic occupancy

Physical occupancy is occupied area divided by leasable area: 89.3% here. Economic occupancy is rent actually collected divided by the rent the center would produce at the rents you believe, fully occupied. They diverge when someone is in free rent, an abatement, or a workout, or when vacant area is large and the occupied area is paying in full.

A third number belongs beside them: collections divided by what was billed. That is delinquency, which is not vacancy. Ask for the aging report. A tenant current on base rent and behind on last year's reconciliation is a credit fact the roll will not show.

**Illustrative gap.** Suppose Cedar Crossing billed $1,111,400 of base rent and collected $1,050,000, and two tenants are inside a free-rent period you failed to notice worth $40,000. The roll still shows the contract rent. Cash does not. Write the concession on the row.

## Rollover

Group expirations by year. A workable planning test is whether any single year holds more than about 15 to 20 percent of area or of rent. Above that, refinancing, leasing cost, and income can bunch. The percentages below use 75,000 square feet as the denominator, so vacant area is visible and the column sums to 100.

| Year from a 2026 close | Who | SF | Share of GLA | Base rent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Year 1 (2027) | Maple Dental, Birch Home | 7,400 | 9.87% | $144,000 |
| Year 2 (2028) | Lantern Cafe | 2,400 | 3.20% | $84,000 |
| Year 3 (2029) | Sample Goods, Cedar Fitness | 12,000 | 16.00% | $237,000 |
| Year 4 (2030) | Pine Outfitters | 6,200 | 8.27% | $136,400 |
| Year 5 and later | Harbor Grocery, Northline Pharmacy | 39,000 | 52.00% | $510,000 |
| Vacant now | D-1 | 8,000 | 10.67% | — |

Year 3 is the year to plan, not the year to notice. National or regional credit with sales you can see is a different renewal conversation from a local tenant with a high occupancy-cost ratio. The anchor's remaining term is the other clock. Harbor Grocery runs to 2032 in this example. If that date were inside three years and the inline leases carried co-tenancy, the risk would not be the grocer's rent line. It would be everyone else's.

## In-place rent and a rent you will defend

In-place rent is on the roll. A comparison rent is what you believe a new tenant would pay for that suite. The gap is upside only if the lease ends inside your hold and the space can be re-leased. A center already at or above your comparison rent is stable until rents fall, and then it is rollover risk.

For each row: (comparison rent per foot − in-place rent per foot) × area. **Illustration:** Maple Dental pays $24. If you would underwrite new dental space at $28, the gap is $4 × 1,800 = $7,200 a year, and the lease ends in year 1, so the gap is near. Harbor Grocery at $11 may be "below" an inline rent and still be the correct anchor rent. Do not mark an anchor to inline rates and call it upside.

Contractual steps are income you do not have to re-lease to get. Map them across the hold. A roll with steps is more predictable than a roll that depends on renewals at a higher rent.

## Recoveries

| Structure | Tenant pays | Owner still carries | Often seen on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Triple net | Base, CAM, taxes, insurance | Structure and capital, as the lease leaves them | Inline and many national tenants |
| Modified gross | Base plus a negotiated slice | The rest | Older leases, some local tenants |
| Gross | Base only | The operating costs | Some legacy deals and some anchors |
| Double net | Base, taxes, insurance | CAM | Some anchors and ground leases |

Recovery ratio is CAM billed divided by CAM incurred. An illustrative band for a mostly triple-net center is 85 to 95 percent. Under 80 percent, look for gross leases, anchor exclusions, and caps. Over 100 percent can be an administrative fee the leases allow. It can also be a billing error. The companion piece, "CAM Reconciliation for Shopping Centers: The Landlord's Complete Guide," is the annual close. On the roll, you only need to see which structure each row uses, and whether an anchor is outside the denominator.

On Cedar Crossing, Harbor Grocery is excluded from the CAM denominator. Every inline share is higher than area ÷ 75,000. Birch Home's share is 5,600 ÷ 45,000 = 12.4%, not 5,600 ÷ 75,000 = 7.5%. That is not a rounding issue. It is the lease.

## Credit is a tier, not a logo

Equal rent is not equal risk. A corporate guaranty from a tenant with public financials is a different row from a single-shop cafe with a personal guaranty. Use tiers so the model can haircut income on purpose.

| Tier | What it means | How to treat it in a model | Illustrative haircut |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| National credit | Corporate guaranty, financials you can read | Count the rent, keep a small vacancy reserve | Minimal |
| Regional credit | Multi-site operator, statements you have seen | Count most of it | A small reserve |
| Local | One shop, personal guaranty | Count it, and reserve more | Heavier |
| Distressed | Delinquent, in workout, or a known closure | Do not underwrite the rent as durable | Deep, or vacant |

The haircuts are a method, not a credit rating. On this fictional roll, Harbor Grocery and Northline Pharmacy can be treated as stronger credit only because the example says so. Sample Goods, Lantern Cafe, Maple Dental, Cedar Fitness, Pine Outfitters, and Birch Home are local or regional names with no real statements behind them. A real file would sort them from documents, not from the sound of the name.

Occupancy cost ratio is total occupancy cost divided by sales, where the lease gives you sales. An illustrative flag for inline tenants is a ratio above the mid-teens. Anchors often sit much lower because the rent per foot is low against their volume. No sales, no ratio. Write "not reported" instead of guessing.

## Mix

Planning bands, not a grading curve:

| Test | A calmer band | A reason to look harder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anchor share of area | About 30–55% | Above 60%, rent density gets thin |
| Inline share of base rent | About 55–75% | Under half, income is anchor-shaped |
| Credit tenants' share of rent | A majority you can describe | A minority, and the rest is local |
| One tenant's share of rent | Under about 25% | Over about 30% |
| Vacant area | Under about 10% | Over about 15% |

Cedar Crossing's anchor is 40% of area. Vacancy is 10.7%. Year-3 rollover is 16% of area. Those three sentences are the start of an investment memo. They are not a conclusion.

If the grocer leaves, do not only delete $330,000. List every inline lease whose co-tenancy fires, and price the reduced rent and any termination right. That total is the anchor exposure. Stress it. A drop in inline sales after an anchor leaves is a scenario, not a fact you can quote as a universal percent.

## Software prepares the roll. It does not underwrite it

A model can extract rows from a spreadsheet, build the expiration table, and flag a gap against comparison rents you provide. It will also misread a date. Check every commencement, expiration, and rent figure against the source file before the output goes into a memo. Side letters and verbal concessions are not in the export. Someone who has read the lease file has to say whether the roll is the deal.

Use the tool to draft the schedule. Approve the schedule yourself.

## Flags worth a second hour

| Test | Illustrative target | Illustrative flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical occupancy | 90% or better | Under 85% |
| Economic occupancy | Near the physical number | A wide gap you cannot explain |
| Recovery ratio | 85–95% | Under 80%, or over 100% without a fee clause |
| Rollover inside 12 months | Under 10% of area | Over 20% |
| Anchor term remaining | More than 5 years | Under 3, with no option |
| Inline occupancy cost | You have sales, and the ratio is modest | Sales missing, or the ratio is high |
| One tenant | Under 25% of rent | Over 30% |
| Below-comparison leases | Quantified | Ignored |
| Delinquency | Under about 3% of billed rent | Over about 5% |

Cedar Crossing fails a strict 90% physical test at 89.3%, and year 3 is inside the "look harder" band at 16%. That is why the example is built this way. A clean roll teaches less.

## The practical next step

1. Tie every row to a lease, an amendment, or a vacancy note. Unmatched rows are not income.
2. Build the expiration table with area and rent, and circle any year over your threshold.
3. Mark comparison rent only where you can say where the number came from.
4. List co-tenancy exposure on the anchor, in dollars.
5. Read the aging report before you believe the roll.
