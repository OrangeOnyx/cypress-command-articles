# A Human Oversight Protocol for AI-Assisted Underwriting

"Prepare, Recommend, Decide: Three Levels of AI Involvement and Who Signs Each" names three levels and stops AI at recommend. "Where Human Review Belongs in an AI-Enabled Workflow" says where a person sits in the flow. What is new here is the step-by-step for a single deal file — an acquisition, a refinance, or a lease decision — and the override record. This is how an owner-led business performs it on an ordinary deal.

Article 14 of the EU AI Act is the provision people cite when they want human oversight designed into a high-risk system: understand the tool, watch it, override it, and be able to stop it. Whether your tool is high-risk, and whether the Act applies to your entity, is counsel's classification. The procedure below is written so you can meet that shape if the answer is yes. If a use is a credit decision about a person, counsel tells you what notice is required. NIST's GOVERN function is a useful checklist for "someone is actually accountable." None of those sentences is a determination that they bind you. Ask, then keep the procedure.

## Where it applies

Any deal file in which a model is consulted, produces an output, or is sitting in the file a person will rely on:

- An acquisition, a refinance, or a lease decision on a property you own or are buying.
- Rent-roll, lease-abstract, tenant, and vacancy review when a model summarized them.
- Guarantor or partner, and entity, financial review.
- Comparable sales, cap rates, and market notes a model assembled.
- Any score, flag, or rating on a deal, a property, or a guarantor or partner.

People bound by it: anyone who submits data, reads an output, or signs a recommendation. Vendors with access to the tool are on the list. A named owner is required.

Review the procedure every year, and sooner if the regulation changes, the model changes, a material finding lands, or the override rate leaves the band you set. An illustrative band that should force a reread is an override rate above 20 percent in a quarter, or below the floor in the risk register, near 2 percent. Both extremes mean the relationship between the person and the model has changed.

## What each role owes

Three roles. Counsel and the CPA are consulted. They are not a fourth line on the grid.

| Role | Who holds it | What they owe |
|---|---|---|
| Owner | The principal | Decides, signs, and approves exceptions. Names who holds the other two roles. |
| Preparer | The staff analyst, asset manager, or outside bookkeeper | Runs the tool and checks citations. Does not sign the decision. |
| Second reader | A partner, the CPA, or the owner on a one-person shop | Reviews deals above a size the owner sets. |

A grid with no names is not allowed. A one-person shop writes the owner's name on two rows. That is allowed.

Competency is a short list, not a certificate program. The person who signs can explain what the model is for, what it does not see, how to override it, and which data never goes in. New users do not sign files until they have done that on a sample file with the owner watching. Refresh on the same annual cycle as the protocol. Keep the attendance, not a transcript.

## The stages, in order

No stage is skipped. The point of the order is to stop anchoring: the person writes a view before seeing the model's view.

**1. Submission.** The preparer assembles the rent roll, trailing statements, entity and guarantor or partner financials as the acceptable-use annex allows, valuation material, market inputs, the terms being considered, and the lease abstracts. A completeness check runs first. Gaps go on a list, not into a silent upload.

**2. Model run.** The system may produce a recommendation (pass, go, stop, conditional, or insufficient data), a confidence indication, the factors it used, the comps it leaned on, and data-quality flags. The output stays hidden until stage 3 is written down. If your tool cannot hide it, complete the independent note in a separate document before opening the output. Honor systems fail. Prefer the software constraint.

**3. Independent view.** Ordinary underwriting, without the model. A preliminary buy, refinance, lease, stop, or conditional, with the reason, dated.

**4. Comparison.** Now open the output. Note where it matches, where it does not, and any factor you do not understand. An output you cannot explain is not "probably fine."

**5. Reconciliation.** If the views differ, or the model flags a risk you did not, write the difference and the reason you are keeping your view or changing it. This is the record "The Audit Trail" is about.

**6. Decision.** The owner decides. The second reader also signs when the deal is above the size the owner set, or when an override scenario below says so. The decision record holds the independent view, the model output, the reconciliation, and the final recommendation.

**7. Sample.** The owner, or the second reader, reads a sample of decisions. An illustrative sample is 20 percent of AI-assisted files in a period, or all of them if the book is small. A cluster of overrides is a reason to ask counsel, not a note you save for year-end.

**8. Rewrite.** Once a year, or on the triggers above. Version the protocol. Say what changed.

## Overrides

An override is allowed. An undocumented override is a missing file. Overrides are not a way around a protected characteristic. The business must be technically able to disregard the model. A tool that will not proceed without accepting its recommendation is not fit for this protocol.

**A. Model says stop/decline, owner says buy or refinance.** Write the factors the model missed or overweighted, and the way you are covering the risks it did flag. The owner signs. If the deal is above the size you wrote down, the second reader signs too.

**B. Model says go, owner says stop.** Write the risk the model missed. The owner signs. A second signature is not required for the stop itself. If the reason could track a protected characteristic, that is scenario C.

**C. The override reason could treat a protected group differently.** Any pattern, or any single file, where the person's reason would treat a protected group differently if repeated. For owner uses — tenant selection, or any residential component — ask counsel. The file includes a short note: who else would this reason touch? Counsel is consulted before the reason becomes a habit.

**D. The output is nonsense, or the system failed.** Do not "work around it" with a partial screenshot. Mark the output unusable, decide without it, and tell the person who runs the tool the same day. If the failure is repeated, halt.

**E. A fact arrives after the model ran.** Rerun if the tool allows a clean rerun. If you proceed on the old output plus the new fact, the record says both, and the person owns the combination.

**F. A policy exception.** The owner approves the exception in writing, with an expiry. Exceptions do not become the new policy by repetition. Three of the same exception in a quarter is a protocol rewrite, not a stack of waivers.

## What the forms hold

You do not need six templates. You need three contents.

**Decision record.** Deal, date, tool name and version, independent recommendation and reason, model recommendation and confidence, differences, final recommendation, signer, whether an override scenario applies.

**Override note.** Scenario letter, facts the model did not have, why the final view is better, second reader when the scenario requires one, and whether you asked counsel.

**Gap list.** What was missing at submission, whether you proceeded, and what you used instead.

The audit trail keeps those three, plus the inputs the model saw and the output it returned, in the deal file. "The Audit Trail" describes the shape. This protocol says they are not optional on an assisted file.

## Quality, escalation, halt

Sample for two things: did the independent note exist before the output was opened, and do overrides cluster on a group, a product, or a person? Clustering is a reason to stop and ask counsel, not a coaching tip you save for year-end.

Escalate the same day when the acceptable-use triggers fire (rank gap, income gap, stale check), when scenario C is even arguable, when the system fails, or when a complaint about the deal names the model.

Halt authority sits with the owner. Halt means outputs stop informing recommendations. In-flight files revert to the independent view alone, and the record says the model was off. Restart only after the cause is written and, if the cause was a biased outcome or a bad training set, after a fresh check.

## What you watch

| Indicator | Why it is there | Illustrative reading |
|---|---|---|
| Override rate | A file that never disagrees may mean the person stopped looking | Under about 2 percent, investigate; a 5–15 percent band is a teaching sign of life, not a target |
| Share of files with an independent note dated before the output | The protocol's main control | Anything under "all of them" is a miss |
| Variance flags open past the date you set | Unread flags | Aging flags are unread flags |
| Sample pass rate | Whether the notes are real | Thin notes count as fails |
| Check age | A stale model escalates | Older than twelve months, stop relying |

The owner reads the log on the cadence written on the protocol. Drift and retraining sit with the person who runs the tool and with "When the Model Changes Under You." This protocol only insists that a drifted model is not quietly still in stage 4.

## Questions an owner asks

**Is the model making the decision?** No. If your files read as if it is, the protocol is not in force, whatever the policy says.

**It broke mid-file.** Scenario D. Manual from there. Tell the person who runs the tool. Do not paste the half-finished output into the memo.

**How do we keep a biased outcome out?** You do not, by hoping. You run a bias check before you rely on the tool, you sample overrides, and you treat a cluster as a reason to stop and ask counsel.

**May I override?** Yes, in writing, on the scenario that fits. Some overrides need the second reader. None of them need a workaround in the software.

**How often does this get reread?** Annually, and when the model, the law, or the override rate moves.

## The practical next step

1. Write the stages on one page and mark the step your current tool cannot enforce. That mark is a system change or a separate note — pick one this month.
2. Add the decision-record fields to the file template. Refuse to close a recommendation that leaves them blank.
3. Name who can turn outputs off. On a one-person shop, that is the owner.
4. Pull five recent assisted files. If the independent view is missing or is dated after the model output, the protocol is still a draft.
5. Put the annual reread on the calendar next to the bias check, not in a separate month you will skip.
